# Normal orientation tensor ("Channel T") for LiDAR degeneracy detection: theory, thresholds, aliasing, Bingham test

Access note: the arXiv HTML of LF-GICP (https://arxiv.org/html/2608.19522) could not be read (the proxy returned HTTP 429, rate limited). Its content below comes only from a third-party review summary (pith.science) and should be checked against the paper before anyone cites it.

## (a) Exact relationship between the normal orientation tensor T and the point-to-plane information matrix (H_tt = Σ n_i n_iᵀ = N·T?)

### Takeaway
Yes. Under point-to-plane linearization with unit weights, the Jacobian row is [(p_i × n_i)ᵀ, n_iᵀ], so the 6×6 Gauss-Newton information matrix is C = Σ [p_i×n_i; n_i][p_i×n_i; n_i]ᵀ. Its translation-translation block is exactly Σ n_i n_iᵀ = N·T (Gelfand et al. 2003; Low 2004; LP-ICP/X-ICP use J_t,i = n_iᵀ). So Channel T is a correspondence-free, rotation-block-free, unit-weight proxy for the H_tt block. It is not an independent signal. It differs from the real H_tt only through (i) which normals are used (scan normals vs matched map-plane normals), (ii) the robust/outlier weights, (iii) the subsampling to 400 points, and (iv) normalization by N.

### Cited Findings
- Gelfand et al. 2003 write the point-to-plane error as Σ_i ((p_i − q_i)·n_i)² and linearize the motion as an incremental [ω, t]. The resulting covariance matrix is C = Σ_i [p_i×n_i ; n_i][p_i×n_i ; n_i]ᵀ, with blocks Σ(p_i×n_i)(p_i×n_i)ᵀ (rotation), Σ(p_i×n_i)n_iᵀ (cross) and Σ n_i n_iᵀ (translation). — [Gelfand, Ikemoto, Rusinkiewicz, Levoy, "Geometrically Stable Sampling for the ICP Algorithm", 3DIM 2003](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)
- Gelfand et al. measure stability with the condition number κ = λ_max/λ_min of C. Small eigenvalues correspond to unconstrained "sliding" directions. To make rotation and translation commensurable, they move the point centroid to the origin and scale so the mean point distance is 1. — [Gelfand et al. 2003](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)
- Low 2004 (UNC TR04-004) minimizes Σ((M s_i − d_i)·n_i)² with a small-angle approximation (sin θ≈θ, cos θ≈1). This gives a linear system Ax=b with x=[α,β,γ,t_x,t_y,t_z]ᵀ. Row i of A is [a_1i, a_2i, a_3i, n_ix, n_iy, n_iz], where a_1i = n_iz s_iy − n_iy s_iz... In Low's notation the (a_1,a_2,a_3) components equal s_i × n_i. The solution is x_opt = A⁺b via SVD. AᵀA therefore has translation block Σ n_i n_iᵀ. — [Low 2004, "Linear Least-Squares Optimization for Point-to-Plane ICP Surface Registration"](https://www.comp.nus.edu.sg/~lowkl/publications/lowk_point-to-plane_icp_techrep.pdf)
- LP-ICP (2025) states that for point-to-plane correspondences the translation Jacobian is J_t,i = (n_i^M)ᵀ, where n_i^M is the map-plane normal, and that its localizability formula becomes identical to X-ICP's under a Lie-algebra rotation parametrization. Per-constraint contribution is F_ji = |J_i v_j|, with rotation and translation analyzed separately (J_r, J_t). — [LP-ICP, arXiv:2501.02580](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02580v1)
- X-ICP (Tuna et al., T-RO 2023/24) evaluates localizability per direction from point-to-plane constraint contributions, with rotation and translation decoupled. — [X-ICP, T-RO](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1109/TRO.2023.3335691); [project page](https://sites.google.com/leggedrobotics.com/x-icp)
- Zhang, Kaess & Singh (ICRA 2016), the degeneracy analysis used by LOAM-family systems, define the degeneracy factor through the smallest eigenvalue of AᵀA. The fetched summary reports "D = λ_min + 1"; this form is unverified and should be checked against the PDF. — [Zhang, Kaess, Singh, ICRA 2016](https://frc.ri.cmu.edu/~zhangji/publications/ICRA_2016.pdf)
- LF-GICP (2026, from a review summary only) builds a "voxel-normal localizability field" M = Σ_v ρ_v n_v n_vᵀ from planarity-weighted map normals. It flags degeneracy with f_0 = λ_min(M)/tr(M), which is the same statistic as Channel T's λ3, and with λ_0 = λ_min(M)/|V|. It argues that GICP covariance regularization keeps the translation block artificially well conditioned in tunnels. — [Pith review of arXiv:2608.19522](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)

### Inferences
- With unit weights and the same normals, N·T = H_tt exactly, so λ3(T) = λ_min(H_tt)/N. Channel T's λ3 test is therefore the Zhang-2016 / X-ICP eigenvalue test restricted to the translation block, normalized by N, and evaluated before data association. Calling it an "independent channel" in a fusion or voting scheme overstates its independence. At most it is a correspondence-free predictor of the H_tt eigen-structure.
- Channel T ignores the rotation block and the rotation-translation coupling Σ(p×n)nᵀ. The smallest eigenvalue of the full 6×6 C (equivalently, of the Schur complement H_tt − H_tr H_rr⁻¹ H_rt) can be smaller than λ_min(H_tt). So Channel T can miss degeneracies that couple rotation and translation, such as a cylindrical tunnel where roll about the axis is unconstrained, or helical/screw motions. For translation, Σ n nᵀ alone suffices. For rotation, Σ(p×n)(p×n)ᵀ is needed. This is standard linear algebra and was not found stated in this exact form in the sources.
- Normalizing by N (trace-1 T) discards the absolute information. A direction constrained by 40 of 400 normals (λ ≈ 0.1) is often perfectly observable in practice, because information grows with the count, not the fraction. In a simulation (own computation, not from a source), a ground-dominated scene with 80% ground / 10% x-wall / 10% y-wall normals at 5° noise gave λ = (0.797, 0.105, 0.097). That is λ3 < 0.15, so Channel T would flag it as degenerate even though every axis is constrained by about 40 normals. A trace-normalized fraction threshold like 0.15 is not a physical observability criterion. f_0 = λ_min/tr in LF-GICP has the same issue, which is presumably why LF-GICP adds the absolute λ_0.

### Gaps
- The Zhang 2016 "λ_min + 1" form and the claim that D is dimensionless were not verified. Eigenvalues of AᵀA depend on N and on units, so the summarizer's "dimensionless" claim looks wrong.
- The LF-GICP paper was not read directly (HTTP 429). Its exact threshold values are unknown.

## (b) The correct statistical and physical way to threshold λ3 (noise floor, N, principled thresholds)

### Takeaway
Two principled routes exist. Neither one is "λ3 < 0.15".
1. **Physical / estimation-theoretic route.** Threshold the absolute information along the weak axis, λ_min(H_tt) = N·λ3 (ideally weighted by 1/σ_r²), against the required pose covariance: σ_t² ≈ σ_r² / λ_min(Σ w_i n_i n_iᵀ). Equivalently, require N·λ3 ≥ σ_r²/σ_t,max².
2. **Statistical route.** Test whether λ3 exceeds the floor that normal-estimation noise produces for a truly 2-D (girdle) normal distribution. That floor is λ3 ≈ σ_n² (per-axis angular normal noise in rad²).

The literature thresholds actually used (Zhang 2016, LP-ICP) are empirical: midpoint calibration or hand-tuned constants. Calling 0.15 "empirical" is consistent with field practice, but it has no physical derivation.

### Cited Findings
- Zhang et al. 2016 choose the degeneracy threshold empirically: "the threshold determining degeneracy is calculated from one test containing both well-conditioned scenes and degenerate scenes… The threshold is set at the mid-point of the margin between both groups." — [Zhang, Kaess, Singh, ICRA 2016](https://frc.ri.cmu.edu/~zhangji/publications/ICRA_2016.pdf)
- LP-ICP uses hand-set thresholds T₁=50, T₂=30, T₃=15, T₄=9 (localizability categories) and filters h_f=0.03 and h_u=0.4998, "based on experimental validation". — [LP-ICP arXiv:2501.02580](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02580v1)
- Gelfand et al. use the condition number κ of the scale-normalized C as the stability measure, which avoids an absolute-unit threshold. — [Gelfand et al. 2003](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)
- The LF-GICP review summary reports that its thresholds on f_0 and λ_0 were "calibrated once on two 500-frame sequences (KITTI 00, GEODE Tunnel01)" and then frozen. The review criticizes this as circular because those sequences also appear in the benchmarks. — [Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)
- Fabric analysis: perfect point, girdle and random distributions correspond to λ1>λ2=λ3, λ1=λ2>λ3 and λ1≈λ2≈λ3 respectively. Woodcock strength is C = ln(λ1/λ3), and Woodcock shape plots ln(λ1/λ2) against ln(λ2/λ3). Vollmer (1990) defines P/G/R indices in [0,1]. Vollmer notes "No attempt has been made to give the indexes statistical significance". — [Vollmer 1990, GSA Bulletin](https://www2.newpaltz.edu/~vollmerf/papers/Vollmer_1990_GSAB.pdf)

### Inferences (own derivation and simulation, flagged as not from sources)
- **Noise floor.** If the true normals lie exactly in a plane (a perfect girdle, e.g., corridor walls and floor, with no normal component along the corridor axis e), and each estimated normal has isotropic tangential angular noise of std σ (rad) per axis, then (n̂·e)² has expectation ≈ σ². So E[λ3] ≈ σ² + O(1/N) sampling bias.
  - Simulation, N=400, corridor with normals ±y and +z: σ = 0°, 2°, 5°, 10°, 15°, 20° gave λ3 = 0.000, 0.001, 0.008, 0.028, 0.062, 0.10. The σ² values are 0, 0.0012, 0.0076, 0.030, 0.069, 0.122.
  - So λ3 = 0.15 corresponds to the noise floor of a perfect corridor only at about 22° per-axis normal noise. That is plausible for k=12 PCA on sparse, ring-structured LiDAR data far from the sensor, but it should be measured rather than assumed.
- **Sampling bias.** The smallest eigenvalue of a sample second-moment matrix is biased downward relative to the population value when eigenvalues are close (eigenvalue spreading). Normal noise biases λ3 upward. With N≈400 the sampling spread is small (order 1/√N on each λ) compared with the 0.15 threshold. The noise bias dominates.
- **PCA normal noise model.** Normal angular error from k-NN PCA scales roughly as σ_range / (neighborhood extent), and it grows with range and with ring-sparse sampling. This rule of thumb is not sourced here. A per-scan σ_n can be estimated from the PCA residual eigenvalue ratio μ3/μ2 of each neighborhood.
- **Principled detector (proposed).** Declare the weak direction v3 unobservable if Σ_i w_i (n_i·v3)² < σ_r²/σ_t,max². Here w_i = 1/σ_r,i² (or robust weights), and σ_t,max is the tolerable translation standard deviation along v3. This uses N·λ3 directly, accounts for N, and maps to metres. Optionally, compare λ3 against the noise floor σ̂_n² + z·SE as a hypothesis test of "girdle + noise".
- **Alternative statistic.** If a dimensionless shape statistic is wanted, the Woodcock ratio ln(λ2/λ3) is better than a raw λ3. It separates girdle (large ln(λ2/λ3)) from point and random distributions, and is invariant to how λ1 is split. It still needs a noise-aware cutoff.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found that gives a closed-form noise floor for λ3 from PCA-normal noise in LiDAR. The σ² result above is our own derivation plus simulation.
- No source was found that derives an absolute λ_min(H) threshold from a target covariance in a LiDAR paper, though this follows from standard Gauss-Newton / Fisher-information arguments.

## (c) Literature on detecting repetitive/self-similar structure (correspondence aliasing) in LiDAR registration

### Takeaway
λ3 of T (any second-moment statistic of normals) is blind to periodicity by construction. A corridor with periodic panels and a smooth corridor can have identical T. Aliasing means a multi-modal registration cost (discrete ambiguity), not a rank-deficient Hessian (continuous degeneracy). The deployed Channel T therefore cannot detect aliasing, and that claim should be removed or backed by an added periodicity term. In this search we found little dedicated literature on detecting periodic aliasing in LiDAR scan-to-scan/map registration. The related work covers (i) sampling-based or multi-hypothesis uncertainty of ICP that captures multi-modality, and (ii) feature-matching work on "ambiguous / repetitive geometry".

### Cited Findings
- Rusinkiewicz & Levoy 2001: normal-space sampling buckets points "according to the position of the normals in angular space, then sample[s] as uniformly as possible across the buckets". It is motivated by small features such as grooves, which alone constrain some DOF ("an inability to determine certain components of the correct rigid-body transformation"). It addresses under-constraint, not periodicity. — [Rusinkiewicz & Levoy, "Efficient Variants of the ICP Algorithm", 3DIM 2001](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~smr/papers/fasticp/fasticp_paper.pdf)
- Gelfand et al. 2003 note that normal-space sampling "put[s] an equal number of points in the flat areas … as it does in the grooves". They propose covariance-eigenvector-based sampling instead, which also addresses continuous instability, not periodic ambiguity. — [Gelfand et al. 2003](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)
- SVN-ICP (2025) estimates ICP-based LiDAR odometry uncertainty with Stein Variational Newton, a particle-based method able to represent non-Gaussian, multi-modal posteriors. — [SVN-ICP arXiv:2509.08069](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.08069)
- Cross-modal feature fusion for point-cloud registration with "ambiguous geometry" (ISPRS J. 2025) targets repetitive or ambiguous geometry by adding image features. This is feature-based global registration, not ICP odometry. — [arXiv:2505.13088](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13088)
- D²-LIO (2025) handles directional degeneracy in LiDAR-IMU odometry. It treats directional (Hessian) degeneracy, not periodic aliasing. — [D²-LIO arXiv:2508.14355](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14355)

### Inferences
- A principled aliasing detector would evaluate the registration cost (or an occupancy/range-image autocorrelation) along the dominant direction(s) v1/v2 of the scene. It would look for secondary minima at offsets d ≠ 0 within the motion-prior window, i.e., a 1-D autocorrelation or Fourier peak at panel period P. Aliasing is dangerous when P is smaller than about 2× the inter-scan motion uncertainty (prior σ from IMU). This is our own proposal; no primary LiDAR source was found for it in this search.
- Because Channel T uses only λ3, the thesis claim "Channel T detects correspondence aliasing" is unsupported by the method itself. It holds only incidentally, when periodic scenes are also corridor-like, i.e., when λ3 is small because of the corridor geometry and not because of the periodicity.

### Gaps
- No dedicated LiDAR paper on periodicity/autocorrelation-based aliasing detection was retrieved within the tool budget. Related computer-vision work on repeated-structure detection (e.g., visual SfM "repetitive structures") and the "perceptual aliasing" literature in place recognition were not fetched.

## (d) Correct form and hypothesis of the Bingham uniformity test, and tests for the "girdle" shape

### Takeaway
The Bingham (1974) test has H0: the axes are uniform on the sphere, i.e., E[nnᵀ] = I/3. On S² the statistic is B_n = (15n/2)(tr(S²) − 1/3) = (15n/2) Σ_i (λ_i − 1/3)², asymptotically χ² with 5 degrees of freedom. Rejecting uniformity says nothing about whether one eigenvalue is near zero. Real LiDAR scenes always reject it: our simulation gives B ≈ 330–630 for corridors and also very large values for well-conditioned scenes, against a critical value of 11.07. Using it as a degeneracy detector tests the wrong hypothesis. The appropriate question is a one-sided test on λ3 (H0: λ3 ≥ λ_crit, or a noise floor), or a girdle-vs-other shape test.

### Cited Findings
- Bingham test: with S = (1/n) Σ U_i U_iᵀ, "When U ∼ Unif(S^{p−1}), then E[UU′] = (1/p) I_p". The statistic is B_n = (n p (p+2)/2)(tr(S²) − 1/p). "Under H₀, B_n is asymptotically distributed as a χ²_{(p−1)(p+2)/2}". For p=3 this gives B_n = (15n/2)(tr S² − 1/3) with 5 df. The original reference is Bingham (1974), Ann. Statist. 2(6):1201–1225. — [García-Portugués & Verdebout, "An overview of uniformity tests on the hypersphere", arXiv:1804.00286](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00286)
- The same survey says the Bingham test "is not consistent against all types of deviations from uniformity. For instance it cannot detect vMF alternatives". It only detects alternatives with E[UUᵀ] ≠ I/p. The Rayleigh test R_n = 2n‖Ū‖² ~ χ²_p detects vMF alternatives but not axial ones. — [arXiv:1804.00286](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00286)
- Equivalent geology form (Vollmer, citing Mardia 1972): uniformity statistic I = 7.5 [(λ1/N − 1/3)² + (λ2/N − 1/3)² + (λ3/N − 1/3)²], with eigenvalues of the unnormalized tensor. n·I equals (15n/2)Σ(λ̂_i − 1/3)² for the normalized eigenvalues λ̂_i. — [Vollmer 1990](https://www2.newpaltz.edu/~vollmerf/papers/Vollmer_1990_GSAB.pdf)
- Shape (girdle vs cluster) descriptors: Woodcock C = ln(λ1/λ3) (strength) and the ln(λ1/λ2) vs ln(λ2/λ3) plot (shape). Vollmer's P, G, R indices and the cylindricity B = 1 − R. Vollmer notes formal significance tests could come from Mardia (1972), Charlesworth et al. (1976) and Kelker & Langenberg (1988). — [Vollmer 1990](https://www2.newpaltz.edu/~vollmerf/papers/Vollmer_1990_GSAB.pdf)

### Inferences
- Own Monte Carlo check (N=400, 2000 uniform samples): the mean of B = (15N/2)Σ(λ_i − 1/3)² was 5.04 (χ²₅ mean 5) and its 95th percentile was 11.37 (χ²₅: 11.07). This confirms the 15N/2 scaling and 5 df for trace-1 T.
- For the corridor simulation, B ranged from 330 to 630. A well-conditioned 80/10/10 scene also rejects strongly. So B separates neither degenerate from non-degenerate scenes, nor scenes from each other, at any practical N. It grows linearly with N for any fixed non-uniform population. This confirms the suspicion in the brief.
- Correct hypothesis framing for degeneracy: H0: v3ᵀ E[nnᵀ] v3 ≥ τ (sufficiently constrained) vs H1: < τ, a one-sided test on the smallest eigenvalue. τ should come from the noise floor (≈σ_n²) or, better, from the physical information requirement in (b) applied to N·λ3. Asymptotic distributions of sample eigenvalues of the orientation tensor are derived in Bingham-distribution inference (Bingham 1974; Mardia & Jupp 2000, ch. 10). We did not retrieve them in this search, so a bootstrap over normals is a practical substitute for computing a confidence bound on λ3.
- Watson and Bingham distribution concentration parameters: a girdle-type Watson (κ<0) or a Bingham distribution with one large negative concentration describes the "corridor" shape. A likelihood-ratio test of the girdle Watson against the Bingham distribution is possible in principle, but would still be a shape test, not an observability test.

### Gaps
- The exact chapter and equation numbers in Mardia & Jupp (2000) and Fisher, Lewis & Embleton (1987) were not retrieved, since the textbooks are not freely accessible. The formula above is confirmed via the García-Portugués & Verdebout survey.
- Anderson–Stephens (1972) tests and formal eigenvalue-ratio tests for "girdle" shape were not retrieved.
- Nubert et al. 2022 ("Learning-based localizability estimation", arXiv:2203.05698) predicts localizability from raw scan data with a network trained only on simulated data, before scan-to-scan registration. It shows that correspondence-free prediction is an established idea. The details of its label generation were not retrieved. — [arXiv:2203.05698](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.05698v2)
