# Channel T novelty check: surface-normal distribution (T = (1/N) Σ n nᵀ, Bingham/Watson) as a LiDAR degeneracy/localizability indicator

Research date: 2026-10-06. Access notes: arxiv.org HTML for LF-GICP (2608.19522) and NV-LIO (2405.12563) returned HTTP 429 (rate limit). LF-GICP details were taken from the arXiv abs page plus the Pith summary page; NV-LIO details came from the ar5iv mirror. The Pith page for 2608.15532 returned 403, and the DNSS PDF on core.ac.uk returned 403. All extraction went through a summarising fetch tool, so check the exact equations against the PDFs before quoting them in the thesis.

## Q1. Has anyone used the eigenvalues of the normal scatter/orientation tensor directly as a degeneracy indicator? How do the results compare with Hessian/eigenvalue methods?

### Takeaway
Yes. This idea has clearly been published. NV-LIO (2024) computes C = (1/m) Σ nₖnₖᵀ over matched normals and flags degeneracy when λ_min < threshold, which is essentially Channel T's core statistic. LF-GICP (arXiv 2608.19522, 2026) uses a weighted voxel-normal scatter M = Σ ρ_v n_v n_vᵀ with f₀ = λ_min/tr(M) and λ₀ = λ_min/|V| as a gated degeneracy detector. Gelfand 2003 and RELEAD (2024) eigen-analyse the translational Hessian block, which for point-to-plane is the same Σ n nᵀ. **None of the papers we checked reports degeneracy-detection accuracy (precision, recall, F1 or AUC) against ground-truth labels.** All of them evaluate downstream odometry error (ATE/RTE) or show qualitative results only.

### Cited Findings
- **NV-LIO** (arXiv 2405.12563, 2024). Computes the covariance matrix of the normal vectors of matched pairs, C = (1/m) Σₖ nₖnₖᵀ, and eigen-decomposes it. "The smallest eigenvalue, λ₀, indicates a degenerate case if it falls below a specific threshold." It also sets the measurement covariance as Q = s·V·diag(1/λ₀, 1/λ₁, 1/λ₂)·Vᵀ and skips loop factors when degeneracy is detected. It reports no quantitative detection accuracy, only qualitative and mapping results. — [NV-LIO (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.12563); [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.12563v1)
- **LF-GICP: Parameter-Free Degeneracy-Aware LiDAR Odometry via a Voxel-Normal Localizability Field** (Eunsoo Im, arXiv 2608.19522, 2026). The localizability field is a 3×3 "regularization-free voxel-normal scatter matrix" M = Σ_v ρ_v n_v n_vᵀ, built from map voxel normals with planarity weights ρ_v and accumulated before covariance regularisation. The paper uses two statistics: the normalised fraction f₀ = λ_min(M)/tr(M) for anisotropy, and the per-voxel mass λ₀ = λ_min(M)/|V| to tell "information absence" apart from "dilution". The minimal eigenvector gives the unobservable translation axis. A temporal-median gate with hysteresis switches on Fisher-information correspondence weighting when f₀ < entry threshold and λ₀ < τ₂ = 0.0143. Baselines are KISS-ICP, VGICP, GenZ-ICP and X-ICP on KITTI, GEODE, MulRan and HeLiPR, with KITTI RTE of 0.865%. There is no Bingham/Watson modelling and no detection accuracy against labels; evaluation is ATE/RTE only. — [arXiv abs 2608.19522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19522); [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)
- **Gelfand, Ikemoto, Rusinkiewicz, Levoy, "Geometrically Stable Sampling for the ICP Algorithm"** (3DIM 2003). Builds the 6×6 covariance C = Σ [p×n; n][p×n; n]ᵀ (its translational block is Σ n nᵀ). Stability is measured by the condition number κ = λ₁/λ₆, and small eigenvalues correspond to "sliding" directions. The paper says normal-space sampling [Rusinkiewicz] "is aimed at constraining translational sliding" and that its own method "deals with both translational and rotational uncertainties." — [Stanford PDF](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf); [Princeton page](https://gfx.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/); DOI 10.1109/IM.2003.1240258 (DOI from memory; confirm on IEEE Xplore)
- **RELEAD** (arXiv 2402.18934, 2024). Splits the point-to-plane Hessian into blocks A′_rr, A′_rt, A′_tt and runs eigen/SVD analysis on the rotation and translation sub-blocks separately to find degenerate directions. A′_tt = Σ n nᵀ, so its translational test is an eigen-analysis of the unnormalised normal scatter. It reports no detection accuracy against labels. — [RELEAD (ar5iv)](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.18934)
- **Zhen & Scherer, "Estimating the Localizability in Tunnel-like Environments using LiDAR and UWB"** (CMU RI, ~2019). Builds position-constraint rows from normals, F = [−nᵢ/(nᵢᵀrᵢ) …], and orientation rows from rᵢ×nᵢ, then projects them into eigenspace and sums absolute row values. It interprets the constraints as "wrenches (forces and torques)." This is a normal-based localizability measure, but a weighted one rather than the plain Σ n nᵀ. — [CMU PDF](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2019/06/root.pdf)
- **LiLi** (Hulchuk, Bayer, Faigl; arXiv 2609.17145, 2026). Uses perturbation and re-optimisation with PCA in se(3), not normals. It compares only against Zhang 2016 and reports alignment error and ATE/RPE, with no precision/recall against labels. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17145v2)
- **Tuna et al., "Informed, Constrained, Aligned"** (arXiv 2408.11809, field analysis). Notes that the literature "lacks a comprehensive analysis of how to effectively use the LiDAR degeneracy information." It compares mitigation strategies; the fetched summary found no precision/recall evaluation of detection. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11809v2)
- **X-ICP** (Tuna et al., arXiv 2211.16335, T-RO). Performs "fine-grained localizability detection" by "analyzing the alignment strength against the principal directions of the optimization." — [abstract mirror](https://awesomepapers.io/robotics/papers/2211.16335); [project page](https://sites.google.com/leggedrobotics.com/x-icp). The exact per-point formula could not be retrieved in this session (see Gaps).

### Inferences
- Using λ_min of T = (1/N) Σ n nᵀ as a translational degeneracy flag is **not novel**. NV-LIO already does exactly this, with the same 1/m normalisation and a λ_min threshold. LF-GICP does a weighted and trace-normalised version (λ_min/tr) with a mass term and temporal gating. Gelfand 2003 and RELEAD eigen-analyse the same matrix as the translational Hessian block. The thesis should cite all four and describe Channel T as building on them, not introducing the idea.
- Normalising by N (or by trace) removes point-count scaling. That is the practical difference between "T" and the raw Hessian block H_tt. LF-GICP explicitly argues that both a normalised quantity (f₀) and an absolute mass quantity (λ₀) are needed. The thesis should address the same issue: T alone cannot tell "few constraints" apart from "anisotropic constraints".
- The possible open niche is **evaluation**. No paper we checked reports detection accuracy (precision/recall/F1/AUC) of a normal-scatter indicator against labelled degenerate frames, or compares it head-to-head with Hessian/Schur-complement indicators. If the thesis provides that, it is a defensible contribution, phrased as "within the papers surveyed".

### Gaps
- The X-ICP per-point formula was not verified this session. From prior knowledge (unverified here), X-ICP projects each point's normal (and p×n) onto the Hessian eigenvectors and aggregates |nᵢ·v_k| as a "localizability contribution". Confirm from the arXiv PDF of 2211.16335 before citing.
- Exact numeric results of NV-LIO and LF-GICP versus Hessian methods were not extracted. Neither paper reports detection accuracy, only odometry or mapping quality.
- "Degenerate in Whose Frame? An Equivariance Condition for Degeneracy Detection in LiDAR Registration" (arXiv 2608.15532) appeared in search results. Its page returned 403, so its content is unverified. It may be relevant to the frame-dependence of the T vs Schur-complement comparison.
- D²-LIO (arXiv 2508.14355) and arXiv 2510.13287 appeared in a search on "normal vectors / degeneracy" but were not read.
- LP-ICP (arXiv 2501.02580) was not read. It extends X-ICP-style localizability to point-to-point and point-to-plane terms.

## Q2. Has anyone fitted a Bingham (or Watson) distribution to scan normals for degeneracy?

### Takeaway
No such paper was found. Bingham distributions in registration are used for **rotation (quaternion) uncertainty**, not for modelling the spread of scan normals as a degeneracy test. Watson/Bingham mixtures on the sphere do exist for modelling surface normals, but in alignment or segmentation contexts, not degeneracy detection. A Bingham/Watson fit to scan normals as a degeneracy statistic therefore appears novel, within the limits of these searches.

### Cited Findings
- **Glover & Popovic, "Bingham Procrustean Alignment for Object Detection in Clutter"** (arXiv 1304.7399, IROS 2013). Uses the Bingham distribution over unit quaternions. The rotation conditional q|t from least-squares alignment is Bingham, and it is fused "with other Bingham distributions from feature orientations" (surface normals and principal curvatures). It performs no degeneracy or stability analysis. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1304.7399); [MIT PDF](https://lis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/glover-iros13.pdf)
- **Srivatsan et al., Bingham-distribution-based linear filter for online pose estimation** (RSS 2017; IJRR 2018). Uses Bingham for rotation and pose uncertainty in registration. — [RSS 2017 PDF](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2017/06/Arun_RSS_2017_871.pdf); [IJRR 2018 PDF](http://biorobotics.ri.cmu.edu/papers/paperUploads/IJRR_2018_BinghamFilter.pdf)
- **Deep Bingham Networks** (Deng et al.). Uses Bingham for uncertainty and ambiguity in pose estimation, not for normals or degeneracy. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347535168_Deep_Bingham_Networks_Dealing_with_Uncertainty_and_Ambiguity_in_Pose_Estimation)
- A search on "Watson OR Bingham mixture model surface normals" returned spherical-mixture alignment work for camera pose estimation ("The Alignment of the Spheres"), but nothing on LiDAR degeneracy. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/329413693_The_Alignment_of_the_Spheres_Globally-Optimal_Spherical_Mixture_Alignment_for_Camera_Pose_Estimation)
- LF-GICP, NV-LIO and RELEAD use the plain second-moment matrix with no directional-statistics fit (see Q1 sources).

### Inferences
- The Bingham maximum-likelihood fit for axial data is driven by the eigen-decomposition of the scatter matrix T: its eigenvectors are T's eigenvectors, and the concentration parameters are monotone functions of T's eigenvalues. Bingham parameters therefore carry largely the same information as eig(T). This is standard directional statistics (Mardia & Jupp), but no source for it was fetched this session; cite it from the book. A novelty claim should rest on what the probabilistic model adds: hypothesis tests for uniformity or girdle vs bipolar shapes (e.g. Bingham's uniformity test), calibrated p-values, or likelihood-based thresholds. It should not rest on simply re-parameterising λ_min.
- A uniformity/isotropy test on normals is a principled threshold-free alternative to the hand-set λ_min threshold in NV-LIO. Of the papers checked, only LF-GICP claims to be parameter-free, and it gets there through a different mechanism (normalised fraction plus mass with temporal gating).

### Gaps
- Google Scholar was not searched directly. A niche paper using Watson/Bingham normal fits for degeneracy (for example in RGB-D SLAM) could exist and not have been found.
- The Mardia & Jupp relation between scatter-matrix eigenvalues and Bingham MLE/uniformity tests was not fetched this session.

## Q3. Established relation between Σ n nᵀ and the point-to-plane translational Hessian / Fisher information

### Takeaway
This relation is well established. For point-to-plane residuals rᵢ = nᵢᵀ(R pᵢ + t − qᵢ), the Jacobian with respect to t is nᵢᵀ. So the Gauss–Newton Hessian (the Fisher information under i.i.d. Gaussian residual noise σ²) has translational block H_tt = (1/σ²) Σ wᵢ² nᵢnᵢᵀ = (N/σ²)·T for unit weights. Cite Gelfand 2003 as the classic source and Hatleskog & Alexis 2024 for a modern explicit statement.

### Cited Findings
- **Hatleskog & Alexis, "Probabilistic Degeneracy Detection for Point-to-Plane Error Minimization"** (arXiv 2410.10784, 2024). Writes the Hessian as H = Σᵢ wᵢ² [[pᵢ]×, I]ᵀ nᵢnᵢᵀ [[pᵢ]×, I], so the translational block is Σ wᵢ² nᵢnᵢᵀ. Points and normals are modelled with Gaussian noise. A direction is "effectively non-degenerate" if the signal is, with high probability, at least an order of magnitude above the noise. There is no comparison against labelled ground truth. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2410.10784); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10784v2)
- **Gelfand et al. 2003.** C = Σ [p×n; n][p×n; n]ᵀ, whose lower-right 3×3 block is Σ n nᵀ; stability is measured by κ = λ₁/λ₆. — [PDF](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)
- **RELEAD.** Writes the same block structure, with A′_tt = Σ n nᵀ. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.18934)
- **LF-GICP.** Describes its normal scatter as the information carrier and turns on "Fisher-information correspondence weighting" — [Pith](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)

### Inferences
- T is exactly the translational Hessian/Fisher block H_tt divided by N (up to σ² and weights). It is **not** the Schur complement H_tt − H_tr H_rr⁻¹ H_rt, which is the marginal translational information once rotation is also estimated. T ignores rotation–translation coupling, so it can overestimate translational constraint when rotation and translation are correlated (e.g. off-centre geometry). Equivalently, T is an upper bound in the Loewner order: H_tt ⪰ Schur complement. This is a precise, well-founded way to justify running a separate "T channel" next to a "Schur channel": T is the conditional (rotation-known) information and the Schur complement is the marginal information. The pairing appears new as a two-channel detector, but the ⪰ relation itself is textbook linear algebra.
- Gelfand also notes the main weakness of normal-only reasoning: normal-space sampling (Rusinkiewicz & Levoy 2001) handles only translational sliding, not rotational. This supports pairing T with a Hessian/Schur channel for rotation.

### Gaps
- Censi 2007 ("An accurate closed-form estimate of ICP's covariance") is a commonly cited source for ICP covariance and Fisher information, but it was not fetched or verified this session.

## Q4. Which claims are novel and which are already known? (Includes the sampling literature.)

### Takeaway
Already known: (a) T = (1/N) Σ n nᵀ and its λ_min as a translational degeneracy test (NV-LIO 2024; LF-GICP 2026; implicitly Gelfand 2003 and RELEAD); (b) its eigenvector as the degenerate translation axis (LF-GICP; Gelfand); (c) T ∝ the translational Fisher/Hessian block (Gelfand; Hatleskog & Alexis); (d) using the spread of normals to ensure translational constraint (normal-space sampling 2001; DNSS 2018 for rotation). Plausibly novel within this search: (1) a Bingham/Watson fit or formal uniformity test on scan normals as a degeneracy statistic; (2) an explicit two-channel design that pairs the conditional block T with the Schur-complement marginal channel and analyses their gap (coupling); (3) a quantitative evaluation of detection accuracy against labelled degeneracy, including T vs Hessian/Schur comparisons, since none of the checked papers reports one.

### Cited Findings
- **Rusinkiewicz & Levoy, "Efficient Variants of the ICP Algorithm"** (3DIM 2001). Introduces normal-space sampling: select points so the normals are spread uniformly over the sphere of directions. — [Princeton page](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~smr/papers/fasticp); [PIXL](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/gfx/pubs/Rusinkiewicz_2001_EVO/index.php); DOI 10.1109/IM.2001.924423 (DOI from memory; confirm)
- Gelfand 2003 on normal-space sampling: it "is aimed at constraining translational sliding" and "takes more iterations to converge since distributing the points equally throughout the sphere of normals puts an equal number of points in the flat areas … as in the grooves." — [PDF](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)
- **Kwok, "DNSS: Dual-Normal-Space Sampling for 3-D ICP Registration"** (IEEE T-ASE, 2018/2019). Extends normal-space sampling to rotational ("dual normal", p×n) space. The full text could not be read (403); content inferred from title and venue. — [IEEE Xplore 8301593](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/8301593/); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/DNSS:-Dual-Normal-Space-Sampling-for-3-D-ICP-Kwok/b66a16b78bbd944b54c9b3f6a1590a7a12465352); [code](https://github.com/eclipse0922/DNSS)
- The absence of detection-accuracy evaluation in NV-LIO, LF-GICP, RELEAD, LiLi, Hatleskog & Alexis, and the Tuna field analysis is documented in the Q1 and Q3 sources above.

### Inferences
- Suggested thesis wording: "Following NV-LIO [2405.12563] and LF-GICP [2608.19522], which threshold the smallest eigenvalue of the surface-normal scatter matrix, and Gelfand et al. [2003], who showed that this matrix is the translational block of the point-to-plane ICP covariance, Channel T uses T = (1/N) Σ n nᵀ. Our contributions are (i) [Bingham/Watson uniformity modelling, if implemented], (ii) pairing T, the rotation-conditioned translational information, with the Schur-complement marginal channel and using their discrepancy to detect rotation–translation coupling, and (iii) the first (to our knowledge) labelled evaluation of normal-scatter vs Hessian-based detectors."
- Do not claim that "using the normal distribution for degeneracy detection" is new; NV-LIO and LF-GICP rule that out.
- If the thesis cites "LF-GICP" as a "voxel-normal localizability field", that matches the verified title of arXiv 2608.19522.
