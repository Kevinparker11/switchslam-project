# Channel T is prior art; the benchmark is not

**The core idea behind Channel T is already published, so the thesis must not claim "surface-normal distribution for degeneracy detection" as new.** NV-LIO (arXiv 2405.12563, 2024) builds C = (1/m) Σ nₖnₖᵀ over matched normals and flags degeneracy when its smallest eigenvalue drops below a threshold. That is Channel T's statistic, down to the 1/m normalisation ([NV-LIO, ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.12563)). LF-GICP (Eunsoo Im, arXiv 2608.19522, Aug 2026) thresholds λ_min/tr of a weighted voxel-normal scatter matrix inside a "voxel-normal localizability field" ([arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19522)). Gelfand et al. 2003 and RELEAD eigen-analyse the same Σ nnᵀ as the translational block of the point-to-plane Hessian ([Gelfand 2003](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf); [RELEAD](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.18934)). Three claims remain open, within the papers checked. The first is a Bingham/Watson uniformity statistic on scan normals. The second is pairing T, the rotation-conditioned translational information, with DCReg's Schur complement, so that the gap between them measures rotation–translation coupling. The third, and strongest, is a labelled detection-accuracy benchmark: no checked real-data paper reports precision, recall or AUC for a degeneracy detector, and only L3E does so, in simulation. The project's own Multi Floor numbers point the same way. T is not identical to H_tt (correlation 0.74–0.80), and it trailed the Hessian/Schur detectors on drift labels. The thesis should therefore present Channel T as one detector under test inside a new benchmark, not as the contribution itself. All four shortlisted datasets have public links. All the baselines except X-ICP, L3E and LP-ICP have usable code. The work still to do is mostly hands-on: download the files, check their timestamps and frames, and write roughly 200 lines of label-generation code.

## Four prior works already use the normal scatter matrix

The novelty check found the same matrix in four places. Two of the papers threshold it explicitly as a degeneracy test, and none of the four evaluates detection against labels.

| Work | What it computes | Detection test | Labelled accuracy reported? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NV-LIO** (2405.12563, 2024) | C = (1/m) Σ nₖnₖᵀ over matched normals | λ₀ < threshold. It also sets the measurement covariance from diag(1/λᵢ) and skips loop closures while degenerate | No. Qualitative and mapping results only ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.12563)) |
| **LF-GICP** (2608.19522, Im, 2026) | M = Σ ρ_v n_v n_vᵀ over map voxel normals, weighted by planarity and built before covariance regularisation | f₀ = λ_min/tr(M) plus mass λ₀ = λ_min/\|V\|, with a temporal-median gate and hysteresis (τ₂ = 0.0143). The min eigenvector gives the weak axis | No. ATE/RTE on KITTI, GEODE, MulRan and HeLiPR ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19522); [Pith summary](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)) |
| **Gelfand et al. 2003** | 6×6 C = Σ [p×n; n][p×n; n]ᵀ, whose translational block is Σ nnᵀ | κ = λ₁/λ₆; small eigenvalues mark sliding directions | No ([PDF](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)) |
| **RELEAD** (2402.18934, 2024) | Separate eigen-analysis of the Hessian blocks A′_rr and A′_tt, where A′_tt = Σ nnᵀ | Per-block eigen/SVD | No ([ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.18934)) |

The identity behind all four is standard. For point-to-plane residuals, the Jacobian with respect to t is nᵢᵀ, so H_tt = Σ wᵢ² nᵢnᵢᵀ. Hatleskog & Alexis write out the full block form ([arXiv 2410.10784](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10784v2)). Several adjacent methods are normal-based but are not Σ nnᵀ thresholds: Zhen & Scherer's tunnel localizability uses weighted normal rows ([CMU PDF](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2019/06/root.pdf)), and normal-space sampling spreads normals over the sphere to constrain translational sliding ([Rusinkiewicz & Levoy 2001](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/~smr/papers/fasticp)). LiLi uses perturbation and PCA rather than normals and also reports no precision/recall ([arXiv 2609.17145](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17145v2)).

**LF-GICP adds useful motivation.** It argues that covariance regularisation in GICP masks translational degeneracy in the Gauss–Newton Hessian, which is why it builds its scatter matrix from regularisation-free normals ([abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19522)). That is the strongest published argument for computing a normal-only channel next to the Hessian. LF-GICP also shows a design point the thesis has to answer. A normalised statistic such as T = (1/N) Σ nnᵀ cannot tell "few constraints" apart from "anisotropic constraints", so LF-GICP pairs it with an absolute-mass term.

### What the thesis can still claim

**No Bingham or Watson fit to scan normals for degeneracy was found.** In registration, Bingham distributions model rotation (quaternion) uncertainty, as in Glover & Popovic's Bingham Procrustean Alignment ([ar5iv 1304.7399](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1304.7399)) and Srivatsan et al.'s Bingham filter ([RSS 2017](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/storage/publications/2017/06/Arun_RSS_2017_871.pdf)). There is a catch. The Bingham MLE for axial data shares T's eigenvectors, and its concentrations are monotone in T's eigenvalues (Mardia & Jupp; not fetched this round). A Bingham fit that only re-parameterises λ_min therefore adds nothing. The claim holds only if the model supplies something a threshold cannot, such as a formal uniformity test with calibrated p-values, girdle-versus-bipolar shape discrimination, or a likelihood-based threshold-free decision.

**The T/Schur pairing is a sound two-channel design.** By the Loewner order, H_tt ⪰ S_tt = H_tt − H_tr H_rr⁻¹ H_rt. So T reports translational information as if rotation were known, while DCReg's Schur channel reports it after marginalising rotation. The gap between the two directly measures coupling. The inequality is textbook linear algebra, but no checked paper uses the pair as a detector.

**The labelled benchmark is the strongest claim.** NV-LIO, LF-GICP, RELEAD, LiLi, Hatleskog & Alexis and the Tuna et al. field study all evaluate through ATE/RTE or qualitatively. The field study states that the literature "lacks a comprehensive analysis of how to effectively use the LiDAR degeneracy information" ([arXiv 2408.11809](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11809v2)). L3E is the only verified paper with per-direction precision and recall: F1 of 0.517 on a held-out tunnel, in Gazebo ([ar5iv 2203.05698](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2203.05698)).

### Our own result already limits what T can claim

On the real Multi Floor log, **T correlates with H_tt/N at only 0.74–0.80**, because T and the Hessian use different weights and correspondence sets. Against drift labels, **T reached AUC of about 0.57–0.71, against 0.76–0.83 for the Hessian/Schur detectors** (both project measurements). Drift labels are weak: they lag the onset of degeneracy and depend on the estimator. Re-scoring against geometry-based labels is the fair test. If T still trails, the honest framing is a negative result, which is still publishable within a benchmark. Because T ignores coupling, it should be expected to lag most on coupling-dominated scenes such as Cave02 and Stairs.

Suggested wording: *"Following NV-LIO and LF-GICP, which threshold the smallest eigenvalue of the surface-normal scatter matrix, and Gelfand et al., who identified this matrix as the translational block of the point-to-plane ICP covariance, Channel T uses T = (1/N) Σ nnᵀ. Our contributions are (i) [a Bingham/Watson uniformity test, if implemented], (ii) pairing T with the Schur-complement marginal channel and using their discrepancy as a coupling measure, and (iii) to our knowledge the first labelled, per-direction, real-data evaluation of normal-scatter versus Hessian-based detectors."*

**A single shared Hessian is mandatory for a fair comparison.** A-LOAM has no degeneracy check, so after the final iteration, assemble one 6×6 JᵀJ from A-LOAM's edge and plane residuals. Then feed that same matrix to ME, FCN, DCReg's Schur modules, DRPM and the Hessian side of Channel T. Compute T itself from the normals of the same matched plane correspondences, so that any T-versus-H_tt difference comes from weighting alone, not from different point sets. No public tool generates per-axis labels from a GT map, so the perturbation oracle has to be written in-house. The pure GT-map Hessian label is cheaper, but it is circular for Hessian-based detectors. Use it only as a second, model-based label.

## Conclusion

The novelty check moves the thesis's centre of gravity. The earlier working assumption, that an orientation-tensor detector had "no published precedent", is wrong: NV-LIO thresholds the same matrix, and LF-GICP builds a parameter-free version of it. What nobody has done is measure whether any of these detectors, normal-based or Hessian-based, actually finds the degenerate directions on real data. Answering that question with map-derived, per-DoF labels is a contribution that does not depend on Channel T winning.

Channel T now has a sharper role. It is the rotation-conditioned half of a conditioned-versus-marginal pair, and LF-GICP's regularisation-masking argument explains why a normal-only channel can see what a regularised Hessian hides. The project's own numbers suggest it will not beat Schur as a standalone detector. The open question is whether the T-minus-Schur gap identifies coupling-driven degeneracy that neither channel flags on its own, and the four datasets above, with coupling-heavy Cave02/Stairs against the clean corridor and building controls, are well placed to answer it.
