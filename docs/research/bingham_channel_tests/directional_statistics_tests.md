# Valid hypothesis tests on axial data / the orientation tensor T for a "smallest eigenvalue too small" (degeneracy) decision

Notation. n_i unit normals, i=1..N; T_hat = (1/N) sum n_i n_i^T, eigenvalues l1<=l2<=l3 (sum = 1), eigenvectors v1,v2,v3; population tensor T = E[nn^T], eigenvalues lam1<=lam2<=lam3. x_j := v_j^T n. Translation information along v is N*v^T T_hat v (point-to-plane Hessian H_tt = N*T_hat).

Evidence labels used below: "Cited" = I read the cited page in this session. "[Recalled]" = standard textbook/paper result I know but could NOT open in this session (books and paywalled papers were not retrievable) - treat as needing verification against the source before it is cited. "[Derivation]" = my own derivation from the cited/recalled ingredients; the algebra was checked for internal consistency; Monte-Carlo checks are labelled "[Own simulation]". Book chapter/section numbers are NOT verified; only "Watson is in M&J ch. 9" is supported (by the Pewsey & Garcia-Portugues review).

## 1. Standard tests (uniformity vs Bingham, Watson bipolar/girdle, equality of eigenvalues, smallest eigenvalue) with formulas and null distributions

### Takeaway
The only fully standard test in the literature that uses T_hat is the Bingham/Rayleigh-type UNIFORMITY test, B = (N p(p+2)/2)(tr(S^2) - 1/p) -> chi2_{(p-1)(p+2)/2} (chi2_5 for p=3); its null (uniform) is the wrong hypothesis for degeneracy, so it is only useful as a sanity baseline. Valid degeneracy tests must be built on the asymptotic law of the eigenvalues of T_hat under a NON-uniform truth (Section 2); for equality of eigenvalues (girdle symmetry, Watson-vs-Bingham) there are Anderson/Bartlett-type chi-square tests, but they do not answer "is l1 below a threshold" and have no standard "l1 <= tau" test in the textbooks I could access.

### Cited Findings
- Bingham uniformity test: Bn := n p(p+2)/2 * (tr(S^2) - 1/p), S = (1/n) sum U_i U_i'; under H0 (uniform on S^{p-1}) Bn is asymptotically chi-square with (p-1)(p+2)/2 df. For p=3 this is (15N/2)(sum l_i^2 - 1/3) = (15N/2) sum (l_i - 1/3)^2 (because sum l_i = 1), chi2_5. This is exactly the project's current B. — [Garcia-Portugues & Verdebout, arXiv 1804.00286](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00286)
- Motivation and weakness of the Bingham test: based on E[UU'] = I/p under uniformity; it is not consistent against alternatives with E[UU'] ~ I/p (e.g. vMF-type unimodal alternatives, "non-unimodal alternatives with E[U] ~ 0" as summarised by the tool). — [arXiv 1804.00286](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00286)
- Gine (1975) test, consistent against ALL axial alternatives to uniformity: Gn := n/2 - [(p-1) Gamma((p-1)/2)^2 / (2 n Gamma(p/2)^2)] * sum_{i<j} sin(Psi_ij), Psi_ij the angle between U_i,U_j; combined with the Ajne test, Fn = An + Gn is consistent against all non-uniform alternatives. O(N^2) cost; again a uniformity test. — [arXiv 1804.00286](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00286)
- Watson and Bingham are the classical axial models; Watson is in Mardia & Jupp (1999/2000) ch. 9. Cutting et al. (2020) studied uniformity tests on S^d against axial alternatives; Figueiredo (2017) proposed bootstrap and permutation versions of a high-concentration test for homogeneity of principal axes of Watson distributions. — [Pewsey & Garcia-Portugues, arXiv 2005.06889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.06889)
- Bingham density f(x; M, K) = c(K)^-1 exp(x^T M K M^T x), K = diag(kappa_1..kappa_{p-1}, 0); parameters estimated from the scatter matrix S: eigenvectors -> principal axes M, eigenvalue differences -> concentration parameters; uniformity statistic proportional to sum (lambda_i - 1/p)^2 with chi-square asymptotics (secondary, calculator documentation page). — [MetricGate Bingham docs](https://metricgate.com/docs/bingham-distribution/)
- Equality of the (p-q) smallest eigenvalues (subsphericity) for PCA: the statistic T_q = variance of the p-q smallest sample eigenvalues satisfies n(p-q) T_q /(2 d^2 sigma_1) -> chi2 / 2 with df (p-q-1)(p-q+2)/2 under ELLIPTICAL distributions with consistently estimable nuisance constants (sigma_1 is a kurtosis-type constant, d a scale); bootstrap from null-satisfying samples is proposed as an alternative to the asymptotics. For p=3, q=1 (last two eigenvalues equal) df = 2. — [Asymptotic and bootstrap tests for subspace dimension (NSF PAR 10345522)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10345522)
- For non-normal parents the Gaussian-theory distribution of sample roots is non-robust: asymptotic marginal and joint laws depend on fourth cumulants and cross-cumulants (Waternaux 1976, Biometrika 63:639). Davis (1977, Aust. J. Stat.) gives the non-normal PCA asymptotics. (Abstract-level only.) — [Waternaux 1976 abstract](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article-abstract/63/3/639/270952); [Davis 1977 listing](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-842X.1977.tb01088.x)

### Inferences
- [Derivation] Null law of T_hat under uniformity. With n uniform on S^2: E[n_a^2 n_b^2] = 1/15 (a!=b), E[n_a^4] = 1/5. Hence sqrt(N)(T_hat - I/3) -> W, symmetric, off-diagonal var 1/15, diagonal var 4/45, diagonal covariance -2/45, i.e. W is the traceless part of a GOE(3) matrix with off-diagonal std sqrt(1/15). Check: (15/2) E tr(W^2) = (15/2)(3*4/45 + 6/15) = 5 = E chi2_5, consistent with the published B. Consequence: under exact uniformity l1 ~ 1/3 - O(0.26/sqrt(N)) (e.g. N=1000: spread ~0.008); the null distribution of l1 under uniformity is the smallest eigenvalue of a 3x3 traceless GOE (tabulate by Monte Carlo). This only matters for "is the scene isotropic", not for degeneracy at tau ~ 0.01-0.05.
- [Derivation] Why B always rejects: for any T != I/3, B/N -> (15/2) sum (lam_i - 1/3)^2 > 0, so B ~ N * const; the test statistic is a noncentral chi2 with ncp = (15N/2) sum(lam_i-1/3)^2. It measures anisotropy, not smallness of lam1.
- [Recalled, M&J 2000] Watson distribution f(x) ∝ exp(kappa (mu^T x)^2): kappa>0 bipolar (lam3 > lam1=lam2), kappa<0 girdle (lam1 < lam2=lam3). Bingham generalises it (kappa_1..kappa_3 distinct, one fixed by normalisation). The classic Watson (1983) / M&J tests are: uniformity vs Watson (score statistic depends on mu^T T_hat mu maximised over mu, i.e. on l3 for bipolar or l1 for girdle; its null law is NOT chi2 because mu is a nuisance parameter that vanishes under H0 - Davies-type problem, Gaussian-GOE extreme eigenvalue), and "Watson vs Bingham" (rotational symmetry) tests. I could not retrieve the exact statistics/df from M&J or Watson (1983); do not cite specific formulas from this bullet.
- [Derivation] Wald test of axial symmetry about v1 (l2 = l3, i.e. "girdle is isotropic in its plane"): let B = V^T T_hat V restricted to the plane perpendicular to v1; d = (B22 - B33, 2 B23); to first order l3 - l2 = ||d||. Under H0 (lam2 = lam3, and fourth moments rotation-invariant in the plane) Var(B22-B33) = Var(2B23) = E[(1-x1^2)^2]/(2N), so Q = 2N ||d||^2 / E[(1 - x1^2)^2] -> chi2_2. Without the symmetry assumption use the sandwich estimate of Cov(d) (cost O(N)). Check: under uniformity E[(1-x1^2)^2] = 8/15 and Var(W22-W33) = 4/15, matching the GOE moments above. This is consistent with the Anderson/Bartlett chi2 df=2 above, but with a non-Gaussian (bounded-variable) variance constant. It tests girdle SYMMETRY, not degeneracy; a corridor (floor+ceiling+two walls) has lam2 != lam3 so it rejects, which is correct and irrelevant.
- [Inference] No test in the above literature has H0: lam1 <= tau. The closest published object is the asymptotic normality of simple eigenvalues of a scatter matrix with a fourth-moment variance (Section 2), from which a one-sided confidence bound is immediate.

- Exact M&J (2000) chapter/section numbers and exact statistics for the Watson/Bingham tests (bipolar vs girdle score tests, Watson (1983) tests, LRT for l2=l3): books not accessible; need verification. Search results only gave secondary pages.
- The Anderson & Stephens (1972) uniformity-vs-Bingham test: the review above cites it but the tool extraction gave no formula; I did not verify whether it is the LRT version of Bn.

## 2. Asymptotics of sample eigenvalues/eigenvectors of T; one-sided bound/test for lam1 vs tau; which direction is sensible; composite null / equivalence framing

### Takeaway
For iid unit vectors with a simple smallest eigenvalue, sqrt(N)(l1 - lam1) -> N(0, sigma1^2), sigma1^2 = Var((v1^T n)^2) = E[x1^4] - lam1^2, estimated in O(N) by the plug-in; so the log-statistic has approximate variance (kurt-1)/N ~ 2/N when x1 is locally Gaussian. This gives a lower confidence bound L for lam1 and the test "reject H0: lam1 <= tau when L > tau" (certify CONSTRAINED), which is the safety-sensible direction because its Type I error (falsely certifying observability) is the dangerous one. Caveat: this needs N_eff, not N, with spatially correlated normals (Section 5): in my simulation naive-N coverage of a nominal 95% bound fell to 68-87%.

### Cited Findings
- Sample roots under non-normality depend on fourth cumulants (Waternaux 1976; Davis 1977 for PCA) rather than the Gaussian 2*lambda^2 variance, and repeated roots need separate treatment. — [Waternaux abstract](https://academic.oup.com/biomet/article-abstract/63/3/639/270952); [Davis 1977](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-842X.1977.tb01088.x)
- Simple eigenvalues (multiplicity one) get a normal limit; multiple eigenvalues give non-normal (GUE/GOE-type) limits requiring separate theory (stated for spiked Wishart; same qualitative dichotomy). — [Statistica Sinica 17 (2007) 1617, Asymptotics of sample eigenstructure](https://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/oldpdf/A17n418.pdf)
- Elliptical-model subsphericity tests, and the bootstrap-from-the-null alternative, are given in [NSF PAR 10345522](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10345522) (see Section 1).

### Inferences
- [Derivation] Matrix CLT (iid): sqrt(N)(T_hat - T) -> W with Cov(W_ab, W_cd) = E[n_a n_b n_c n_d] - T_ab T_cd. Delta method / first-order perturbation for a simple eigenvalue: l_j ≈ lam_j + v_j^T (T_hat - T) v_j, so sqrt(N)(l_j - lam_j) -> N(0, sigma_j^2), sigma_j^2 = Var(x_j^2) = E[x_j^4] - lam_j^2 (no normality used, only that n is bounded). Plug-in: sigma1_hat^2 = mean((v1_hat^T n_i)^4) - l1^2. [Recalled] this matches the finite-fourth-moment form in Davis (1977)/Waternaux (1976); Anderson (1963) gives the Gaussian case 2 lam^2; I could not read the formulas.
- [Derivation] Small-lam1 regime: if x1 ≈ N(0, lam1) locally (planes with small noise, Bingham with strongly negative concentration), E[x1^4] = 3 lam1^2 so sigma1^2 = 2 lam1^2: Var(log l1) ≈ 2/N. Equivalent chi-square form N l1/lam1 ≈ chi2_N, giving the lower bound lam1 >= N l1 / chi2_{N; 1-alpha}. Use the empirical kurtosis K1 = mean(x1^4)/l1^2 (instead of 3) so Var(log l1) ≈ (K1 - 1)/N; heavy-tailed x1 (outlier normals, clutter) inflates it.
- [Derivation] One-sided bound and test. L = l1 - z_{1-alpha} sigma1_hat / sqrt(N_eff) (or the log form l1 * exp(-z sqrt((K1-1)/N_eff))). Test A: H0: lam1 <= tau (degenerate) vs H1: lam1 > tau (constrained); reject iff L > tau. The size is controlled at the boundary lam1 = tau (power function monotone in lam1), giving a composite-null test of size alpha; rejecting = positive certification of observability. Test B (reverse): H0: lam1 >= tau vs H1: lam1 < tau; reject iff upper bound U < tau (certify degeneracy; alpha controls false degeneracy alarms, which in SLAM only cost accuracy/robustness by triggering conservative handling). Sensible direction: choose the null to be the state whose wrongful rejection is costlier; for localisation safety that is Test A (null = degenerate). Because lam1 is a continuum, use TWO thresholds tau_deg < tau_ok (an "equivalence/indifference zone" in the sense of TOST: constrained if L > tau_ok, degenerate if U < tau_deg, otherwise "uncertain -> keep other channels"). The interval-based three-way decision is equivalent to running both one-sided tests.
- [Derivation] Boundary/bias issues. l1 = min_u u^T T_hat u is a minimum of unbiased estimators, so E[l1] <= lam1 (Jensen); second-order bias ≈ -(1/N) sum_{j=2,3} E[x1^2 x_j^2]/(lam_j - lam1) ≈ -2 lam1/N for lam1 << lam_j (relative bias -2/N_eff). Nearly repeated small eigenvalues (lam1 ≈ lam2, e.g. normals concentrated around a single axis: "point" fabric) make the denominator blow up and the normal approximation fails; in that case bound the SUM l1 + l2 (smooth, simple functional), or use a fixed-direction test u^T T_hat u (a plain sample mean of y_i = (u^T n_i)^2, standard CLT/t-test, no eigen-problem). The normal approximation for l1 also ignores the estimation of v1; it is a second-order effect for a simple lam1.
- [Own simulation, python/numpy, 3000 reps per cell, iid girdle model: x1~N(0,s^2) (clipped), rest uniform on the circle, truth lam1 = s^2, lam2=lam3] Coverage of the nominal 95% one-sided LOWER bound for lam1 (target >= 0.95): delta-method bound l1 - 1.645*sigma_hat/sqrt(N): N=50: 0.985; 100: 0.98; 300: 0.975; 1000: 0.959; 10000: 0.950 (same numbers for lam1 = 0.001, 0.01, 0.05 within +-0.005 - because the relative variance is scale-free, as the derivation predicts). Log form with fixed sqrt(2/N): 0.976, 0.973, 0.968, 0.953, 0.949 at the same N. E[l1]/lam1 = 0.958 (N=50), 0.979 (100), 0.992 (300), 0.998 (1000), 1.000 (10000), matching the -2/N bias. So under independence the asymptotic bound is valid (slightly conservative) from N ~ 50 upward for a single well-separated small eigenvalue; the limiting factor in practice is dependence, not N.
- [Inference, important framing] For the CURRENT scan, l1 is the realised information (H_tt = N T_hat, translation information per axis = N l1 / sigma_p^2 if range noise sigma_p); there is no sampling uncertainty about the realised Hessian. A hypothesis test is meaningful only for a superpopulation question: (i) would the constraint persist for the next scan/pose, or if a few patches (doors, clutter, dynamic objects) were absent; (ii) is the apparent info coming from a handful of non-parallel patches that dominate l1; (iii) normal-estimation noise biases T_hat toward isotropy (adding noise to normals adds ~ eps*I/3-type mass to l1, so a true degenerate plane-only scene looks less degenerate; this bias is upward, so Test A is anti-conservative with noisy normals unless noise is modelled). Use absolute information N l1 (not only the fraction l1) when the threshold must reflect sensor noise.
- [Derivation, proposal] Non-asymptotic alternative from matrix Chernoff [Recalled, Tropp 2012, "User-friendly tail bounds for sums of random matrices", Thm 1.1; not retrieved here]: for iid PSD summands with largest eigenvalue <= R=1 (n n^T has norm 1), P(lambda_min(sum n n^T) <= (1-delta) N lam1) <= d [e^{-delta}/(1-delta)^{1-delta}]^{N lam1}, d=3, which behaves like 3 exp(-delta^2 N lam1 / 2). It is distribution-free for iid data but conservative, and requires N_eff*lam1 >> 1 (e.g. N_eff*lam1 >= ~20-50) to be informative; useful as a sanity check that the asymptotic bound is not being used where N_eff*lam1 is O(1).

### Gaps
- Could not read Anderson (1963), Davis (1977), Waternaux (1976) bodies or Watson (1983); the sigma_j^2 = E[x_j^4] - lam_j^2 formula is my derivation (standard delta method), not a quote.
- No literature found that explicitly tests "smallest eigenvalue of an axial orientation tensor below a threshold" for LiDAR degeneracy; the Test A/Test B construction and the equivalence framing are my inference.
- Behaviour when lam1 ≈ lam2 (near-multiplicity): only qualitative (GOE/GUE limit); no usable closed-form correction found.

## 3. Bingham parameters, MLE, normalising constant, LRT for girdle; per-frame cost

### Takeaway
Bingham is a regular exponential family whose sufficient statistic is T_hat; the MLE of the axes is the eigenvectors of T_hat and the MLE of the concentrations solves E_kappa[x_j^2] = l_j. Therefore the concentrations are a monotone reparametrisation of (l1,l2,l3): a fitted-kappa "degeneracy statistic" carries no information beyond the eigenvalues, and a Bingham LRT is a function of l_j that additionally needs the normalising constant (saddlepoint or holonomic-gradient evaluation) - borderline for per-frame use and unnecessary for the l1 decision, which the delta-method bound of Section 2 provides at 3x3-eigen cost.

### Cited Findings
- Density f(x|theta) = C(theta)^-1 exp(sum theta_i x_i^2), C(theta) = integral over S^{p-1}; one-dimensional contour-integral representation C(theta) = C(0)/(2 pi i) * integral prod_k (-theta_k - t)^{-1/2} e^{-t} dt; Kume-Wood saddlepoint approximation of C works "very well in a range of parameter values" but is not exact; holonomic gradient method gives exact computation and better MLE. — [Sei & Kume, arXiv 1304.7973](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.7973)
- Kume & Wood (2005, Biometrika 92:465-476): writes the Bingham/Fisher-Bingham normalising constant as an elementary function times the density of a linear combination of non-central chi-squares and gives first- and second-order saddlepoint approximations plus a modified second-order variant (slightly better). Implementation: R `Directional::fb.saddle(gam, lam)` returns first-, second- and third-order approximations of log C. — [Kume & Wood 2005 (IDEAS)](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/biomet/v92y2005i2p465-476.html); [Directional package docs](https://www.rdocumentation.org/packages/Directional/versions/4.5/topics/Saddlepoint%20approximations%20of%20the%20Fisher-Bingham%20distributions)
- Kume & Sei (2018): exact evaluation of the Fisher-Bingham normalising constant via the holonomic gradient method; the Bingham-specific MLE using it: [Sei & Kume arXiv 1304.7973](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.7973); [Pewsey & Garcia-Portugues arXiv 2005.06889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.06889). A later paper (Statistics and Computing, 2021, "Maximum likelihood estimation of the Fisher-Bingham distribution via efficient calculation of its normalizing constant") exists: [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11222-021-10015-9) (title only seen).
- Moment-based fitting: eigenvectors of S estimate principal axes; concentrations approximated as functions of eigenvalue differences; "consistent but may be inefficient for small samples or high concentrations" (secondary). — [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/bingham-distribution/)

### Inferences
- [Derivation from exponential-family theory; the MLE equations are [Recalled] Bingham (1974, Ann. Statist. 2:1201) and M&J] Log-likelihood per point = sum_j kappa_j l_j - log C(kappa) (axes at their MLE); the score equations are d log C/d kappa_j = l_j = E_kappa[x_j^2]; the map kappa -> (E x_j^2) is a bijection from {kappa_1<=kappa_2<=kappa_3=0} onto the open ordered eigenvalue simplex. So kappa_hat_1 is a monotone function of (l1,l2,l3) with no extra information; its sampling variance is the delta-method transform of Section 2 (Fisher information = covariance of x_j^2 under the fitted Bingham).
- [Derivation] Large-concentration (girdle) limit by Laplace: with kappa_3=0 (or after shifting), x1 ≈ N(0, 1/(2(kappa_3 - kappa_1))) so lam1 ≈ 1/(2 |kappa_1 - kappa_3|); kappa_1 ~ -1/(2 lam1), e.g. lam1 = 0.01 -> kappa_1 ≈ -50. [Recalled] Kent (1987, Appl. Stat. 36:... "Asymptotic expansions for the Bingham distribution") covers this regime; not verified.
- [Derivation] LRT of "girdle" structures: H0: kappa_2=kappa_3 (Watson girdle) vs Bingham: 2N[ sum (kappa_hat_j - kappa_tilde_j) l_j - log C(kappa_hat) + log C(kappa_tilde) ] -> chi2_1 (one parameter restricted; compare Section 1's chi2_2 Wald test for the plane-symmetry, which has different df because it also tests the rotation of axes in the plane). The l1-threshold LRT: H0: kappa_1 >= kappa0 (restricted MLE on the boundary) is a one-sided boundary problem with the signed-root LRT ~ N(0,1) asymptotically; it is equivalent to a Wald/delta test on l1 up to higher-order terms, with the constrained MLE needing solutions of 1-2 nonlinear equations.
- [Inference, unverified cost estimate] Per frame, Bingham MLE = 3x3 eigendecomposition (<1 microsecond-ish) + solving 2 moment equations for kappa with Newton iterations, each needing log C and its gradient via a 1-D saddlepoint root-find (tens of microseconds in compiled code, 5-20 iterations) -> ~0.1-1 ms in C++; in R/Python the Directional package is orders of magnitude slower. I found no published per-call timing; treat as an estimate, and it buys nothing over the O(N) delta-method bound for the l1 decision.
- [Inference] Where a Bingham fit adds value: goodness-of-fit of a Bingham model, or generating a parametric bootstrap/null (simulate from the fitted Bingham: Kent-Ganeiber-Mardia rejection sampler [Recalled, 2013/2018 - not verified]) to calibrate l1's distribution without asymptotics. With spatially clustered normals, a Bingham iid parametric bootstrap would again have the wrong (iid) dependence.

- Per-evaluation timing for Kume-Wood saddlepoint or holonomic gradient not found.
- Bingham (1974) and Kent (1987) not accessible; MLE equations and Kent's asymptotic expansion are recalled, not verified. The "Wikipedia" page gave no MLE detail.
- No published LRT specifically "kappa_1 below threshold" for degeneracy.

## 4. Structural-geology shape/strength descriptors: tests or only descriptors? sample-size bound

### Takeaway
Woodcock (1977), Vollmer (1990) are descriptors of eigenvalue shape, not tests; the only associated "tests" (e.g. Woodcock & Naylor 1983 randomness test using C = ln(S1/S3)) are Monte-Carlo-calibrated for uniformity, again the wrong null. Vollmer's R = 3*S3 is exactly 3 x the smallest eigenvalue, so thresholding R is the descriptive form of the l1 criterion. No sample-size lower bound is published for the asymptotic eigenvalue results in the sources found; my simulation indicates iid validity from N ~ 50-100, so the binding constraint is the number of effectively independent patches.

### Cited Findings
- Woodcock (1977) with ordered eigenvalues S1>=S2>=S3: shape K = ln(S1/S2)/ln(S2/S3) (K>1 cluster, K<1 girdle, K~1 transitional); strength C = ln(S1/S3) ("randomness", C=0 uniform). — [Geological Magazine, magnetic shape fabric paper](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/25DAA8C00B6178AA81D0E177A7DC5C7B/S0016756822000747a.pdf/magnetic_shape_fabric_analysis_from_syntectonic_granites_a_study_based_on_the_eigenvalue_method.pdf)
- Vollmer (1990): P = S1 - S2 (point), G = 2(S2 - S3) (girdle), R = 3 S3 (random), P+G+R = 1, plotted on a ternary diagram. — same source.
- Secondary shape ratio (lam1 - lam2)/(lam1 - lam_p) with thresholds >0.7 clustered, <0.3 girdle (descriptive). — [MetricGate](https://metricgate.com/docs/bingham-distribution/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] In the user's ascending notation (l1<=l2<=l3): R = 3 l1, G = 2(l2 - l1), P = l3 - l2. Degenerate-translation criterion "l1 small" = "R small"; for an ideal corridor (girdle) R ≈ 0 and G > 0; plane-only normals give P ≈ 1, R=G=0. Uncertainty for these indices follows from the delta method of Section 2 (R: sd = 3 sigma1/sqrt(N_eff)); no new null distribution.
- [Recalled] Fisher, Lewis & Embleton (1987, ch. on axial data) and Woodcock & Naylor (1983, J. Struct. Geol. 5:539-548) treat K, C as descriptors with simulated percentage points for randomness tests; Fisher-Lewis-Embleton also give bootstrap/asymptotic eigenvalue methods; none verified here. State only that they are descriptive/Monte-Carlo.
- [Own simulation] Asymptotic eigenvalue bound for a single small simple eigenvalue under iid sampling was valid or conservative at all N tested (50 to 10,000). I did not test near-repeated eigenvalues or heavy tails; there small-sample failure will be worse.

### Gaps
- Primary texts (Woodcock 1977, Vollmer 1990, Woodcock & Naylor 1983, Fisher-Lewis-Embleton 1987) not accessed; only a downstream paper's restatement of the formulas.
- No published minimum-N rule for Anderson/Watson eigenvalue asymptotics for axial data found.

## 5. Dependence: effective sample size, design effect, block/spatial resampling for directional data

### Takeaway
Spatially clustered normals make naive-N inference badly anti-conservative; for a 3x3 scatter the practical fix is to compute the bound from CLUSTER (patch/voxel/segment) means of y_i = (v1^T n_i)^2 - i.e. batch-means/cluster-robust variance - or equivalently a cluster bootstrap/jackknife, and to use K clusters (or N_eff = N*sigma_iid^2/sigma_LR^2) in place of N. I found no directional-data-specific theory for ESS of spherical samples; the generic dependent-data resampling literature applies to the vector statistic y_i.

### Cited Findings
- General resampling literature for dependent/spatial data exists (block bootstrap, automatic block-length selection: [Politis & White 2004](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Politis_White_2004.pdf); comparison of block and semi-parametric bootstraps for spatial variance estimation: [Computational Statistics & Data Analysis](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167947310002434); Annals of Statistics 34(4) 2006 resampling for spatial regression: [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-34/issue-4/Resampling-methods-for-spatial-regression-models-under-a-class-of/10.1214/009053606000000551.pdf)); these are for scalar/real-valued spatial data, not shown to address axial sphere data. Pewsey & Garcia-Portugues state bootstrap-based tests and permutation versions are increasingly used in directional statistics. — [arXiv 2005.06889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.06889)
- I found no source dealing with effective sample size or spatial dependence for orientation tensors specifically (searches returned only generic block-bootstrap material).

### Inferences
- [Derivation] Long-run variance of the eigenvalue estimator: l1 ≈ lam1 + mean(y_i - lam1), y_i = (v1^T n_i)^2, so sqrt(N)(l1 - lam1) has asymptotic variance sigma_LR^2 = sum_h Cov(y_0, y_h) (spatial sum), and the design effect DE = sigma_LR^2/sigma_iid^2, N_eff = N/DE. Estimate by batch means: partition points into K spatial cells (voxels, scan-line segments, planar-segment labels), y_bar_c per cell, se^2 = Var_c(y_bar_c)/K (with cell sizes weights if unequal); valid if cells are large enough to absorb the correlation and K >= ~20-30. This is a standard batch-means/cluster-robust estimator [Recalled: Kunsch 1989; Politis & Romano subsampling; Lahiri 2003 book - not verified here].
- [Own simulation] Clustered model: K patches each with one normal drawn from the girdle model (lam1 ≈ 0.0104) and m points per patch with isotropic noise sd 0.02; nominal 95% lower bound, 3000 reps. Coverage using naive N: K=10,m=200 (N=2000) 0.77; K=30,m=100 0.71; K=100,m=30 0.68; K=300,m=10 0.75; K=1000,m=3 0.87. Coverage using the cluster-based se (K cells = true patches): 0.994, 0.990, 0.975, 0.970, 0.967. Mean ratio se_iid/se_cluster = 0.075, 0.104, 0.189, 0.327, 0.592 ≈ sqrt(K/N)-ish growth, i.e. design effect ≈ m for m=200..30. So with N in the thousands and K only tens of patches, N_eff ≈ K, an order of magnitude or two below N.
- [Inference, proposal] Cheaper per-frame robustness statistic: since T_hat = sum_c S_c (S_c = 3x3 per-cell scatter sums), leave-one-cell-out values l1^(-c) = lambda_min(T_hat - S_c) cost O(1) each (3x3 eigen) and give a jackknife standard error SE_jack^2 = ((K-1)/K) sum_c (l1^(-c) - mean)^2 plus the worst-case "min_c l1^(-c)" ("would the constraint survive losing any one structure?"). This also handles the case where l1 is supplied by a few dominant patches.
- [Inference] A permutation (null-of-independence) test is not applicable for H0: lam1 <= tau because the null is composite and not exchangeable under spatial dependence; use bootstrap/jackknife.

### Gaps
- No directional-statistics source on effective sample size or spatial block bootstrap for axial data; block length selection for 3D point clouds (irregular, non-stationary) is undocumented.
- Cell definition (voxel size, segment labels) strongly affects K; nothing published for LiDAR normals; the simulation assumes exchangeable patches.

## 6. Bootstrap tests for eigenvalues of T (Fisher & Hall; Fisher, Hall, Jing & Wood 1996)

### Takeaway
Fisher & Hall (1990/1991) and Fisher, Hall, Jing & Wood (1996, JASA 91:1062-1070, "Improved pivotal methods for constructing confidence regions with directional data") are the canonical bootstrap references for directional data: they construct pivotal (studentised) statistics for mean directions/axes that improve coverage over naive bootstrap, but I could not read them and cannot confirm they treat eigenvalue confidence bounds; the generic percentile-t bootstrap of l1 (or log l1) is an inference from the framework.

### Cited Findings
- The papers exist as: Fisher, Hall, Jing & Wood, "Improved Pivotal Methods for Constructing Confidence Regions With Directional Data" (JASA) and Fisher & Hall, "Bootstrap confidence regions for directional data" (listed on ResearchGate; fetch failed with HTTP 429, so content not read). — [ResearchGate: Improved Pivotal Methods](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/246432776_Improved_Pivotal_Methods_for_Constructing_Confidence_Regions_With_Directional_Data); [ResearchGate: Bootstrap Confidence Regions](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/254287158_Bootstrap_Confidence_Regions_for_Directional_Data)
- Context: a related paper by the same line of work (Improved confidence regions for a mean vector under general conditions, Comput. Stat. Data Anal.) and a 2022 review (Scealy, Directions Old and New, Int. Stat. Rev.) appear in search results but were not readable. — [ScienceDirect listing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167947305002781); [Wiley review listing](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/insr.12481)
- Bootstrap-from-the-null alternatives for eigenvalue-structure tests, and permutation/bootstrap tests for Watson axes homogeneity, are used in the literature cited in Sections 1 and 5. — [NSF PAR 10345522](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10345522); [arXiv 2005.06889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.06889)

### Inferences
- [Recalled, not verified] Fisher & Hall's axial-data work bootstraps the orientation matrix and uses the eigenvector (principal-axis) confidence cone; Fisher-Hall-Jing-Wood's improvement is a studentised/pivotal bootstrap with asymptotic coverage error O(N^-1) vs O(N^-1/2). Do not cite these specifics without checking.
- [Derivation, proposal] Percentile-t bootstrap of the eigenvalue bound: resample cells (cluster bootstrap) with replacement, recompute T_hat*, l1*, sigma1_hat*, form t* = (l1* - l1)/se*, and use the (alpha) quantile q_alpha of t* to give L = l1 - q_{1-alpha} se. Variance-stabilised alternative: resample log l1 (Var ≈ (K1-1)/N_eff). Cost: B x (3x3 eigen) after pre-aggregating per-cell 3x3 scatter sums S_c: B = 200-1000 resamples is ~ K x B 3x3-matrix additions + B eigendecompositions, sub-millisecond to few ms - feasible per frame. Bootstrap for a minimum eigenvalue is not guaranteed consistent when the eigenvalue is repeated (non-smooth functional), so prefer the log-scale and check lam1 is well separated (l2 - l1 >> se).
- [Inference] The pivotal improvement matters most for small effective sample sizes (K ~ 10-30), exactly the regime in Section 5, where first-order normal bounds are a bit conservative in my simulation (cluster-based coverage 0.97-0.99 vs 0.95 nominal).

### Gaps
- Fisher & Hall (1990/1991) and Fisher-Hall-Jing-Wood (1996) were not readable (ResearchGate 429), so whether they cover eigenvalue (shape/strength) confidence bounds, and their exact algorithms, is unverified.
- No published bootstrap specifically for the smallest eigenvalue of an axial orientation tensor found.
