## 1. Gelfand/Rusinkiewicz et al. 2003 and Rusinkiewicz-Levoy normal-space sampling: exact criterion and thresholds

### Takeaway
Gelfand et al. (3DIM 2003) use the 6x6 matrix W = sum of [(p x n)(p x n)^T, (p x n)n^T; n(p x n)^T, n n^T], which is exactly the point-to-plane Gauss-Newton Hessian, and the condition number kappa(W) = lambda_max/lambda_min as the stability measure, with the goal "keep kappa as close to 1 as possible". They give no numeric degeneracy threshold: it is a sampling objective (greedy selection until a target sample count), not a detector. Normal-space sampling is described there as handling translational sliding only.

### Cited Findings
- [Published, cited] Gelfand, Ikemoto, Rusinkiewicz, Levoy, "Geometrically Stable Sampling for the ICP Algorithm", Fourth International Conference on 3D Digital Imaging and Modeling (3DIM), October 2003. — [PIXL page](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/index.php), [PDF](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/stabicp.pdf)
- [Published, cited] (extraction of Eq. 4) The matrix is W = sum_i [ (p_i x n_i)(p_i x n_i)^T , (p_i x n_i) n_i^T ; n_i (p_i x n_i)^T , n_i n_i^T ], the covariance of "constraint forces and torques"; n_i is the force direction and p_i x n_i the torque. Eigenvectors are "general screw motions" (rotation about an axis plus translation along it); small eigenvalues relative to lambda_max mark "sliding" directions that cause little error increase. — [Gelfand 2003 PDF](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/stabicp.pdf)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Stability measure: kappa(W) = lambda_max / lambda_min; objective "to keep kappa as close to 1 as possible". Reported examples: kappa 66.1 reduced to 3.7 (incised plane) and 26.9 reduced to 4.1 (sphere). — [Gelfand 2003 PDF](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/stabicp.pdf)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Greedy algorithm: form c_i = (p_i x n_i, n_i); keep six lists sorted by |c_i . X_k| for the eigenvectors X_k; maintain running totals sum |c_i . X_k| and pick the next point from the list whose total is smallest ("the most unconstrained eigenvector"). No stopping threshold: it runs to a target sample count. Overhead is 3-5x per iteration versus uniform sampling. — [Gelfand 2003 PDF](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/stabicp.pdf)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Torque/lever-arm normalisation: translate the points so their centre of mass is at the origin and scale so the average distance of p_i from the origin is 1. Rationale given: this "equalizes the maximum amount of displacement that can be contributed by a point due to torque (rotation) to the amount due to force (translation)". — [Gelfand 2003 PDF](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/stabicp.pdf)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) On normal-space sampling (Rusinkiewicz and Levoy): "Normal-space sampling takes more iterations to converge since distributing the points equally throughout the sphere of normals puts an equal number of points in the flat areas of the patches as it does in the grooves"; it addresses translational sliding only, whereas covariance sampling handles both translation and rotation. — [Gelfand 2003 PDF](https://pixl.cs.princeton.edu/pubs/Gelfand_2003_GSS/stabicp.pdf)

### Inferences
- [Inference] The normal-space sampling idea (uniform occupancy of the Gauss sphere, binned) is the sampling-side analogue of "make T approximately I/3". It is a design heuristic to improve conditioning, not a hypothesis test, and Gelfand et al. argue it is inferior to W-based selection because it ignores torques. This is a useful citation for why a normals-only statistic is incomplete for the rotation block.
- [Inference] Gelfand's centroid-and-unit-scale normalisation is an early, ad hoc fix for the same frame/scale dependence that Zhang et al. (2026, Section 2 below) formalise. The translation block N*T is untouched by that normalisation, so T-based tests are insensitive to the choice of origin.
- [Inference] Rusinkiewicz and Levoy's original paper ("Efficient variants of the ICP algorithm", 3DIM 2001) was not opened in this session; only Gelfand's description of it was.

### Gaps
- Did not open the Rusinkiewicz and Levoy 2001 paper itself, so no direct quote on its bucket count or sampling parameters.
- No numeric kappa cut-off exists in Gelfand et al.; a "threshold" cannot be cited from them.

## 2. Detection statistics, nulls and threshold-tuning status (survey table in prose)

### Takeaway
Almost every published detector is a function of the Gauss-Newton Hessian H (or J), and almost every threshold is empirical (mid-point of margin, basin-of-convergence, sweep, or hand-set). The only thresholds with an explicit statistical story are Switch-SLAM (but the project's arithmetic check shows the chi-square use is flawed), Hatleskog and Alexis (signal-versus-noise probability) and, for frame/scale portability, Zhang et al. 2026 (equivariant metric with thresholds from analytic geometries). Pre-solve normal-based statistics exist in SuperLoc (ICRA 2025, threshold 0.2, empirical), ICET (NAVIGATION 2024) and LF-GICP (arXiv 2026, lambda_min(M)/tr(M), thresholds frozen from two 500-frame sequences).

### Cited Findings

Per-method entries (statistic; null/justification; threshold tuning status):

- **Zhang, Kaess, Singh, "On degeneracy of optimization-based state estimation problems", ICRA 2016.**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Degeneracy factor defined as D = d(delta x)/d(x_c*) (stiffness of the solution to disturbance of an inserted constraint); closed form D = lambda_min + 1 with lambda_min the smallest eigenvalue of A^T A; D depends on A but not b. Threshold: "set at the mid-point of the margin between both groups", from the lambda_min distributions of well-conditioned and degenerate scenes in one sample dataset; no numeric value stated; no special treatment of rotation vs translation scaling; paper treats the generic linear least squares problem and does not derive the point-to-plane Hessian. Status: empirical, dataset-calibrated. — [Zhang 2016 PDF](https://frc.ri.cmu.edu/~zhangji/publications/ICRA_2016.pdf)
- **Hinduja, Ho, Kaess, "Degeneracy-Aware Factors with Applications to Underwater SLAM", IROS 2019.**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Statistic: condition number lambda_max/lambda_min of the ICP A^T A matrix (A built from source points and corresponding normals), with an eigenvalue threshold lambda_t recomputed at every iteration rather than fixed; the authors note that "different types of environments benefit from different threshold values, indicating the need to calculate the threshold from properties of the sensor data". Status: adaptive but heuristic; no hypothesis test. — [Hinduja 2019 PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~kaess/pub/Hinduja19iros.pdf)
- **X-ICP: Tuna, Nubert, Nava, Khattak, Hutter, IEEE T-RO 2024 (arXiv 2211.16335v4).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Per-correspondence "alignment" values in [0,1] from projecting torque-normalised terms (p x n divided by its norm, for rotation) and normals n (for translation) onto the eigenvectors V_r and V_t of the rotation and translation Hessian blocks (absolute value, element-wise); contributions summed per eigen-direction; three categories {full, partial, none}; thresholds kappa_1 = 250 (full vs partial), kappa_2 = 180, kappa_3 = 35 (below: non-localizable); contribution filter kappa_f = cos(80 deg) for Velodyne, cos(60 deg) for Ouster (sensor noise); strong-contribution cos(45 deg). Authors say thresholds are "set based on the basin of convergence of the employed ICP algorithm", kappa_3 "is user-defined" (localizability risk), and the same values are used for all environments and sensors in the paper. Uses map-frame normals; correspondences are transformed back to the LiDAR frame to remove map-size scale effects on rotational eigenvectors. Status: empirical, user-defined, claimed to be environment-independent. — [X-ICP arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.16335v4), [X-ICP HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.16335v4), [T-RO listing](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/10328716)
- **Tuna et al., "Informed, Constrained, Aligned: A Field Analysis on Degeneracy-aware Point Cloud Registration in the Wild", arXiv 2408.11809 (2024).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Field comparison (legged robot, excavator, handheld; tunnels, open fields, construction sites). Review statement: "using only the minimum eigenvalue of the optimization Hessian is impractical due to the required tuning of thresholds"; mentions a chi-squared-based non-heuristic normalised-eigenvalue threshold (Switch-SLAM) and minimum eigenvalue combined with partial condition numbers; concludes that active optimisation mitigation is needed when external odometry is unavailable. — [Field analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11809v2)
- **LP-ICP: Yue, Xu, Chen, Pan, Chen (arXiv 2501.02580; venue listed as T-RO in the extraction, year and venue not confirmed).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Localizability contribution = squared projection of the correspondence Jacobian onto Hessian eigenvectors (point-to-plane and point-to-line); states X-ICP's formula is a special case; empirical thresholds h_f = 0.03, h_u = 0.4998, T_1..T_4 = 50, 30, 15, 9, T_5 = 15; h_f "can be adjusted based on the noise characteristics of the LiDAR". — [LP-ICP](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02580v1)
- **Switch-SLAM: Lee et al., IEEE RA-L 2024 (DOI 10.1109/LRA.2024.3421792).**
  - [Published, cited] Statistic: the three smallest eigenvalues of H_l (6x6), normalised to unit L2 norm. Null: each normalised eigenvalue "follows its respective expectation within 95%"; test (lambda - e_m)^2 / e_m > 0.103 with "0.103 denotes the Chi-squared value for 2-DOFs at a 95% confidence level"; expectations e_m from a semicircle-law argument and min/max reasoning: e = [0.289, 0.498, 0.749]; threshold lambda_t = e_m - sqrt(0.103 * e_m) giving [0.12, 0.27, 0.48]. Authors present this as "non-heuristic" and say the chi-squared value is the only user-defined parameter. In their own comparison: accuracy 0.91 (Zhang), 0.96 (Nashed et al.), 0.96 (proposed); recall 0.91, 0.96, 0.99; they note comparators are "sensitive to threshold tuning". — [Switch-SLAM PDF](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/7083369/10561888/10582434.pdf)
  - [Inference, arithmetic verified] The chi-square (2 dof) CDF is 1 - exp(-x/2), so CDF(0.103) = 0.0502: 0.103 is the lower 5% point, not the upper 95% critical value (5.991). Substituting 5.991 into Eq. 12 gives negative thresholds, so the rule could never fire. Also, Pearson's statistic needs categorical counts, and a single continuous eigenvalue plugged into (lambda - e)^2/e does not follow chi-square; the semicircle law is for symmetric matrices with independent zero-mean entries, whereas J^T J is positive semidefinite. This critique is the project's own (the project documents state they found no published critique); it is not published. Thresholds therefore remain effectively hand-chosen midpoints.
  - Related-work statements inside Switch-SLAM: Zhang et al. and Nashed et al. "rely on a heuristic threshold for the eigenvalues"; Nashed et al. use "the ratio between each eigenvalue and maximum eigenvalue in 3-DOF"; Ren et al. use a degeneracy indicator based on fluctuation of the optimisation vector and need the full optimisation. — [Switch-SLAM PDF](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/iel8/7083369/10561888/10582434.pdf)
- **DCReg: Hu, Chen, Jia, Wu, Tan, Waslander (arXiv 2509.06285; v3 revised March 2026; the project's copy is the IJRR-formatted v3).**
  - [Published, cited] Schur-complement decoupling of H into S_R = H_RR - H_Rt H_tt^-1 H_tR and S_t = H_tt - H_tR H_RR^-1 H_Rt; detection by condition number of each Schur block; Theorem on invariance of kappa(S) to unit rescaling of the eliminated block; Loewner ordering S_R <= H_RR and bound kappa(S_R) <= lambda_max(H_RR) / (lambda_min(H_RR) - lambda_max(M_R)) when lambda_min(H_RR) > lambda_max(M_R) (M_R the coupling matrix). — [DCReg arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06285), [DCReg HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06285v2)
  - [Published, cited] Threshold provenance (important correction to project notes): the sweep in Fig. 19 is over the PCG-preconditioner target condition number kappa_tg (values 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100) on the parking-lot dataset, showing ATE and CD stable for kappa_tg 1 to 10 while PCG iterations rise; the text then says "in practice, we set kappa_tg equal to the detection threshold kappa_th (kappa_th = 10), which lies in the flat region of the curve". So kappa_th = 10 is set equal to a mitigation target chosen from a flat region; it was not calibrated by a detection-accuracy sweep. The paper claims the threshold "is determined solely by eigenvalue ratios across different dimensions, eliminating the need for case-by-case parameter optimization". (Source text: project copy of 2509.06285v3, Section 7.5 and Fig. 19.) — [DCReg arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06285)
  - [Published, cited] DCReg reports that full-Hessian ("FCN") detection over-detects, minimum-eigenvalue ("ME") detection under-detects, and diagonal-block analysis misses coupled degeneracies (stairs and a parking lot with a 10 m range limit); ME fails because rotational eigenvalues are typically larger than translational ones. (Fig. 14 and Fig. 15 text, project copy of v3.) — [DCReg arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06285)
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) DCReg's related work groups "Hu et al. 2024b" (cited as "PALoc: advancing SLAM benchmarking with prior-assisted 6-DoF trajectory generation and uncertainty estimation") with full-Hessian condition-number methods that "fail to disentangle scale disparity and rotation-translation coupling". I could not confirm that this is the "relative condition number" reference the task refers to, nor its venue. — [DCReg HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.06285v2)
- **Hatleskog and Alexis, "Probabilistic Degeneracy Detection for Point-to-Plane Error Minimization", arXiv 2410.10784 (accepted Oct 2024; journal/conference venue not confirmed).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Models noise in points (from sensor datasheet) and in estimated normals, propagates it into the Hessian, and defines a soft classification: probability that the signal is "at least an order of magnitude higher than the noise". Explicitly critiques eigenvalue thresholds: Hessian eigenvalues "depend on the number of measurements, weights, the scale of the environment and sensor noise". Code: github.com/ntnu-arl/drpm. Status: the closest published example of a noise-aware, non-arbitrary test; the factor of ten is still a chosen constant. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10784v2)
- **SuperLoc: Zhao et al., ICRA 2025 (arXiv 2412.02901), "The Key to Robust LiDAR-Inertial Localization Lies in Predicting Alignment Risks".**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Pre-optimisation statistic built from scan geometry: aggregate point-plane Jacobians J_i = [p_i x n_i, n_i]^T into the observability matrix C_w = sum [(p x n)(p x n)^T, ...; ..., n n^T]; then "statistically counts how many point pairs constrain each DoF"; normalised confidence gamma in [0,1] per DoF; degradation flagged when any element of the diagonal covariance is below 0.2 ("high likelihood of degradation"). Claims thresholds need no tuning because the metric is relative in [0,1]. Contrasts itself with post-optimisation Hessian methods ("too late", numerically unstable). The extraction notes there is no direct detection comparison against X-ICP or Zhang; only localisation-accuracy tables. — [SuperLoc arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02901), [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02901)
- **ICET: McDermott and Rife, NAVIGATION (ION) 71(2), 2024 (arXiv 2306.08690).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Weighted least squares with P = (H^T W H)^-1, W from voxel-level point distributions; a pre-solve eigenvector-based sigma-point test on reference-scan voxels excludes measurement directions dominated by extended structure such as walls: if the distribution along a principal axis extends past two voxel boundaries the direction is excluded (aperture problem). Status: geometric rule with a fixed sigma-point cutoff. — [ICET NAVIGATION](https://navi.ion.org/content/71/2/navi.647), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.08690)
- **LF-GICP: Eunsoo Im, "Parameter-Free Degeneracy-Aware LiDAR Odometry via a Voxel-Normal Localizability Field", arXiv 2608.19522 (Aug 2026, preprint; read via a secondary Pith synopsis because the arXiv HTML fetch was rate-limited).**
  - [Published, cited] (preprint, secondary summary) M = sum_v rho_v n_v n_v^T (planarity-weighted map normals); statistics f_0 = lambda_min(M)/tr(M) (scale-free anisotropy) and lambda_0 = lambda_min(M)/|V| (absolute per-voxel mass, to separate "no information" from "dilution"); a temporal-median gate with thresholds tau_on, tau_off, tau_2 triggers Fisher-information correspondence weighting. Central claim: covariance regularisation in voxelised GICP adds an isotropic floor to the translation block of the Hessian and masks translational degeneracy, whereas reading the raw map normal field "before regularization" avoids that masking. Thresholds fixed from two 500-frame sequences (KITTI 00 and GEODE Tunnel01) then frozen; the reviewer notes thin separation margins (0.013-0.017 for lambda_0; tau_2 = 0.0143 leaves under 0.002 to the absence boundary). No mention of Bingham, Watson or orientation tensor. — [Pith synopsis](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)
- **GenZ-ICP: Lee, Lim, Han, IEEE RA-L 2024/25 (arXiv 2411.06766; the PDF header reads "IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, accepted November 2024"; the fetch tool labelled it T-RO, so the venue is flagged as conflicting).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) No explicit detector. Adaptive weight alpha = N_pl/(N_pl + N_po) between point-to-plane and point-to-point terms, with planarity from local covariance eigenvalues (lambda_3/(lambda_1+lambda_2+lambda_3) < tau_planar, "empirically determined per dataset"); stability argued via the condition number of the translational Hessian block (point-to-point has condition number one; point-to-plane fails in corridors "due to imbalanced normal distributions"). — [GenZ-ICP HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06766v1)
- **DAMM-LOAM (arXiv 2510.13287, 2025) and D^2-LIO (arXiv 2508.14355).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Both are Hessian-eigenvalue based: DAMM-LOAM takes eigenvalues of H_tt, normalises to [0,1] and projects point normals on eigenvectors to get per-point contributions; its ground/wall/roof classification thresholds (about 66.7% of neighbouring normals, 15 degrees) have no ablation. D^2-LIO eigen-decomposes A^T A with separate rotation and translation eigenspaces because of scale disparity and uses an inverse-eigenvalue-weighted regulariser. Venues not confirmed for either. — [DAMM-LOAM](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13287v1), [D^2-LIO](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.14355v1)
- **LiLi: Hulchuk, Bayer, Faigl, arXiv 2609.17145 (2026; the paper is anonymised for review; venue not confirmed).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Perturbation-and-re-optimisation detector (axis-aligned perturbations scaled by local density, median point displacement versus tau_displacement, PCA filter tau_PCA, parameter k). Claims Zhang's static-correspondence Hessian method is limited because it ignores re-association; reports open-cylinder error 0.382 m (Hessian) versus 0.037 m (LiLi) and success on a 430 m tunnel round trip where Zhang's method failed. — [LiLi HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17145v2)
- **Zhang, Zhao, Lin, Guo, Jia, Liu (Hebei University of Technology), "Degenerate in Whose Frame? An Equivariance Condition for Degeneracy Detection in LiDAR Registration", arXiv 2608.15532 (2026).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Frame independence requires the metric to transform by congruence, M(T.D) = Ad(T)^T M(D) Ad(T), so that the generalised eigenproblem H v = lambda M v is frame-invariant (necessary and sufficient). Violators: Zhang 2016 (M = I_6, unnormalised spectrum), characteristic-length scaling, block scaling, Jacobi preconditioning; the extraction says X-ICP and DCReg are "implied" violators through block scaling (I could not verify that claim; treat as the summary's inference). Satisfier: the point-displacement metric M = sum_i J_i^T J_i (J_i here the point-displacement Jacobian), yielding dimensionless eigenvalues in [0,1], "the fraction of induced surface displacement that the point-to-plane residual observes". Evidence: only that metric gives 100% label agreement across four transfer scenarios (body-frame change, rescaling, scene-scale change, cross-sequence); at lever arms of 0.032-0.552 m binary labels flip on 0.1-1.0% of frame pairs and applied pose corrections differ on 44.5-69.5% of pairs; thresholds derived from analytically known degenerate geometries (rectangular corridor = 1 direction, circular tunnel = 2, corner = 0). Recommends reporting degeneracy as a subspace of se(3) with dimensionless eigenvalues, not per-axis flags. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15532)
- **GEODE benchmark (arXiv 2409.04961; authors not extracted).**
  - [Published, cited] (extraction) Dataset and evaluation of seven SLAM methods on degeneracy-focused sequences (translation-only, rotation-only and combined degradation); concludes current methods "fail to correctly identify the direction of degradation in point cloud alignment"; no new detection statistic. Useful as a validation dataset. — [GEODE](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04961v1)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Threshold-tuning status summary. Empirical or user-defined: Zhang (mid-margin on one dataset), Hinduja (per-iteration heuristic), X-ICP (basin-of-convergence), LP-ICP, SuperLoc (0.2), DCReg (kappa_th = 10 set equal to a mitigation target in a flat region), GenZ-ICP (tau_planar), DAMM-LOAM, LF-GICP (two-sequence calibration). Derived with an explicit statistical argument: Switch-SLAM (flawed as shown above), Hatleskog and Alexis (signal-to-noise probability, with a chosen 10x factor), and Zhang et al. 2026 (thresholds from analytic geometries, plus an invariance proof). No paper found that calibrates a detection threshold against a null distribution of the statistic (for example a bootstrap or asymptotic null of lambda_min).
- [Inference] Hessian eigenvalues scale with N (point count), weights, point noise and scene scale (this is Hatleskog and Alexis's point), so any absolute lambda threshold in the literature is only valid for a fixed pipeline. A normalised statistic such as lambda_min(T) = lambda_min(H_tt)/N removes the N dependence but not the information-versus-noise question.
- [Inference] The translation-only quantity T has three useful invariances: it does not depend on the origin of p (normals only), it is invariant in spectrum to rotation of the body frame (T -> R T R^T), and it is scale-free. The rotation block depends on p_i x n_i and therefore on the origin and lever arms; that is exactly the frame/scale dependence analysed by Zhang et al. 2026 and handled ad hoc by Gelfand's centroid normalisation and X-ICP's torque normalisation.

## 3. Bingham, Watson, Kent, axial statistics, orientation tensor, structure tensor, Gauss map, extended Gaussian image for degeneracy, localizability, feature selection or place recognition

### Takeaway
I found no LiDAR/registration paper that uses a Bingham, Watson or Kent distribution, or a distributional hypothesis test on the axial normal distribution, for degeneracy or localizability detection. The closest relatives are (i) the unnormalised/normalised "normal matrix" statistics that are the orientation tensor in everything but name (SuperLoc's C_w, X-ICP, LF-GICP's M = sum rho n n^T with lambda_min/tr(M)), and (ii) Straub et al.'s use of a Bingham-to-vMF approximation for surfel normals in a direction-aware SLAM. This is a negative search result, bounded by the queries run (see Gaps), and is relevant for a novelty claim only if worded carefully ("to our knowledge", "in the sources we searched").

### Cited Findings
- [Published, cited] Straub, Cabezas, Leonard, Fisher, "Direction-Aware Semi-Dense SLAM" (arXiv 1709.05774; venue/year not stated in the extraction): surfel normals modelled with a Dirichlet-process von Mises-Fisher mixture; an intermediate Bingham distribution is approximated by a vMF for tractability; the paper argues that "a diverse set of observed plane orientations provides better constrain[t] of the point-to-plane cost function" and uses directional segmentation for guided observation selection during tracking. This is the nearest conceptual precedent (distribution on normals to choose well-conditioned observations) but uses vMF/Dirichlet-process, not a Bingham/Watson degeneracy test, and is RGB-D. — [arXiv 1709.05774](https://arxiv.org/html/1709.05774)
- [Published, cited] Madaan, Suresh, Kalra, CMU 16-833 course report "Exploring Applications of Bingham Distribution for SLAM" (undated, likely 2016-17): quaternion Bingham filter for rotation estimation and Bingham mixture models for surface-normal segmentation; the segmentation result was "inconclusive"; proposes Bingham in planar SLAM measurement models. Course project, not peer reviewed; no degeneracy detection. — [report PDF](https://ratneshmadaan.github.io/files/slam_bingham_course_project.pdf)
- [Published, cited] Search hits for Bingham in robotics are about quaternions, not normals: Glover et al. "Monte Carlo Pose Estimation with Quaternion Kernels and the Bingham Distribution" (RSS 2011) and "Bingham Procrustean Alignment for Object Detection in Clutter" (IROS 2013). — [RSS 2011 PDF](https://www.roboticsproceedings.org/rss07/p15.pdf), [Glover IROS 2013](https://lis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/glover-iros13.pdf), [ar5iv 1304.7399](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1304.7399)

### Inferences
- [Inference] The project's Channel T (T and its eigenvalues) is conceptually the 3D analogue of the Harris/Shi-Tomasi structure tensor (the second-moment matrix of gradient directions) and is mathematically H_tt/N; the published state of the art already uses this object (LF-GICP, SuperLoc, X-ICP projections). What appears unpublished is (a) the interpretation as the scatter matrix of an axial (Bingham/Watson) sample and (b) proper inference on its smallest eigenvalue. Novelty claims should rest on (b) and on the calibrated test, not on "using normals".
- [Inference] What the Bingham/Watson family can legitimately offer: the Bingham (and Watson) parameters are functions of T's eigen-decomposition; a Dimroth-Watson girdle fit (negative kappa) corresponds to normals concentrated on a great circle, i.e. a rank-2 T and a sliding direction along the girdle axis. That connection is real but I found no publication using it for localizability. [Background, unverified here] Bingham 1974 (Annals of Statistics 2(6)), Watson 1965 (Biometrika 52), Mardia and Jupp 2000 "Directional Statistics" are the standard references for these models.
- [Inference] Bingham's uniformity test statistic (for p = 3, S = 7.5 N (tr(T^2) - 1/3) ~ chi-square with 5 dof under uniformity; critical value 11.07 at 5%) [Background, consistent with the project's notes and with Mardia and Jupp Section 10.7; the formula itself was not re-read from text in this session]. Why it is the wrong test for degeneracy: (1) the null is uniformity, but a perfectly conditioned scene (three orthogonal planes, T = I/3) gives S = 0 and is not "non-uniform"; (2) a well-conditioned but anisotropic T such as diag(0.5, 0.3, 0.2) gives S that grows linearly in N and is "significant" for any N of practical size, although lambda_min = 0.2 is far from degenerate; (3) the statistic is a symmetric function of all eigenvalues (sum of (lambda_i - 1/3)^2), so it cannot distinguish "one eigenvalue near zero" from "one eigenvalue large"; (4) point-wise normals from a LiDAR scan are spatially dependent, so the effective N is much smaller than the point count and the nominal chi-square null is anti-conservative. A degeneracy test needs a null on lambda_min itself (for example H_0: d^T T d <= epsilon_0 for some direction d), one-sided, with epsilon_0 tied to a precision or noise requirement.

## 4. Alternatives to eigenvalue thresholds: GDOP analogy, Shi-Tomasi/Harris, Fisher information/observability, statistical rank tests

### Takeaway
Three alternatives have direct support in the sources found: a GDOP-style precision metric (published for GNSS; analogy to LiDAR is my inference), the Shi-Tomasi minimum-eigenvalue criterion (the LF-GICP statistic lambda_min/tr is its 3D analogue), and Fisher-information/noise-aware tests (Hatleskog and Alexis; ICET). Statistical rank tests (Bartlett sphericity, Anderson, Cragg-Donald, Kleibergen-Paap) come from statistics/econometrics; I found no robotics use and could not verify the texts in this session, so they are listed as candidates only.

### Cited Findings
- [Published, cited] Dilution of precision: GDOP is the factor by which geometry multiplies ranging error in position error, computed from the geometry (design) matrix; subsets exist (PDOP, HDOP, VDOP, TDOP). — [Wikipedia: Dilution of precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilution_of_precision), [Trimble GDOP note](https://help.fieldsystems.trimble.com/tbc/2383.htm)
- [Published, cited] No LiDAR scan-matching paper using a GDOP formulation was found in a targeted search (results were GNSS, wireless sensor networks and a LiDAR terminal-approach performance paper). — [search results, e.g. scanning-LiDAR performance envelope](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0030399226013605)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) ICET propagates P = (H^T W H)^-1 with W from measured voxel covariances, i.e. a Fisher-information/covariance characterisation, and applies a pre-solve eigenvector test that excludes wall-dominated directions. — [ICET](https://navi.ion.org/content/71/2/navi.647)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Hatleskog and Alexis define degeneracy through a noise-aware probability that signal exceeds noise by a factor of ten, after propagating point and normal uncertainty into the Hessian. — [arXiv 2410.10784](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10784v2)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Zhang et al. 2026 propose dimensionless eigenvalues of the generalised problem H v = lambda M v with M = sum J_i^T J_i (point-displacement metric), which gives an interpretable [0,1] "observed fraction of surface displacement" scale and frame/scale-portable thresholds. — [arXiv 2608.15532](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15532)
- [Published, cited] GenZ-ICP argues stability via the condition number of the translational Hessian block (point-to-point gives 1; point-to-plane fails with imbalanced normals). — [GenZ-ICP](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.06766v1)

### Inferences
- [Inference] GDOP analogy for translation: with unit-weight point-to-plane residuals of variance sigma^2, Cov(t_hat) = sigma^2 (N T)^-1, so the "translation dilution of precision" is sqrt(tr(T^-1) / N) and the axis-wise standard deviation along eigenvector v_k is sigma / sqrt(N lambda_k(T)). This converts the lambda_min threshold into an interpretable accuracy requirement (for example sigma_t <= 2 cm), which is a physically meaningful alternative to dimensionless cut-offs. It requires an estimate of sigma and of effective N. The project's D1 formulation (sigma_r / sqrt(lambda - mu) <= tau) is of this type.
- [Inference] Shi-Tomasi criterion: accept a feature when the smaller eigenvalue of the structure tensor of gradients exceeds a threshold. Applied to normals it is lambda_min(T) > theta (LF-GICP's f_0 form is lambda_min/tr). [Background, unverified here] Shi and Tomasi, "Good features to track", CVPR 1994; Harris and Stephens, "A combined corner and edge detector", Alvey Vision Conference 1988 (Harris uses det - k tr^2, which is smooth in both eigenvalues). The same caveat as everywhere applies: the thresholds there are also empirical.
- [Inference] Rank tests as alternatives. The degeneracy question is "is the information matrix numerically rank-deficient in direction d" which maps to testing the smallest singular values of a data matrix, i.e. a rank test. [Background, unverified here] Candidates: Anderson (1951, Annals of Mathematical Statistics 22) likelihood-ratio rank test; Bartlett's sphericity/eigenvalue-equality test (1950s; tests equality of eigenvalues, so for T it is again a uniformity-type null and shares the Bingham problem); Cragg and Donald (1993, Econometric Theory 9) minimum-eigenvalue statistic with weak-instrument critical values (Stock and Yogo 2005); Kleibergen and Paap (2006, J. Econometrics 133) generalised rank test based on the SVD, valid under heteroskedasticity. Of these, Cragg-Donald/Kleibergen-Paap (minimum-eigenvalue statistics with calibrated critical values for "weakly identified" directions) are the closest in spirit to a valid degeneracy test; their assumptions (independent observations, regular covariance structure) would need adaptation to spatially correlated LiDAR normals (cluster or block bootstrap by plane/voxel).
- [Inference] Practical valid test recipe for Channel T (not published as such; it combines the pieces above): (1) per scan, compute T and its eigen-decomposition; (2) the null for direction d is H_0: d^T T d <= epsilon_0, one-sided; (3) obtain the sampling distribution of d^T T d (or lambda_min) by a cluster/block bootstrap over planar segments or voxels (not over points), or by delta-method with an effective sample size; (4) set epsilon_0 from a precision requirement (GDOP-type, sigma/(sqrt(N_eff) sqrt(epsilon_0)) <= tau) or from the Hatleskog-Alexis signal-versus-noise criterion; (5) report the dimensionless subspace, not per-axis flags (Zhang et al. 2026). Calibrate on analytically known geometries (corridor, cylinder, corner) as done in Zhang et al. 2026, and validate on GEODE-type sequences.
- [Inference] For rotation, normals alone are insufficient: H_RR = sum (p x n)(p x n)^T needs p. A rotation-side pre-solve statistic must use torques (Gelfand W, SuperLoc C_w, X-ICP torque normalisation) with an origin-independent, equivariant metric (Zhang et al. 2026) or Schur-marginalised block (DCReg).

### Gaps
- Textbook statements for Shi-Tomasi, Harris, Anderson, Bartlett, Cragg-Donald, Kleibergen-Paap and Stock-Yogo were not verified against the original papers in this session (no inline URL, hence only in Inferences). Please verify the exact titles, years and critical-value tables before citing.
- No robotics paper using any statistical rank test for degeneracy was found.
- No LiDAR GDOP paper was found; the analogy is mine.

## 5. Evidence: raw-scan (pre-solve) geometry versus solver-Hessian (post-solve) detection

### Takeaway
There is no paper I found that directly benchmarks a pre-solve raw-normal statistic against the post-solve Hessian statistic on the same data and quantifies where they disagree. Indirect and claim-level evidence: SuperLoc (pre-solve, claims earlier warnings, no direct detection comparison), LF-GICP (argues the Hessian can be masked by regularisation and that map normals before regularisation are more faithful; thresholds thinly separated), ICET (pre-solve voxel test), and LiLi/Hatleskog and Alexis/Zhang et al. 2026 (arguments that Hessian-based thresholds depend on correspondences, N, weights, scale and frame). The expected sources of difference (edge features, robust weights, correspondence rejection, map versus scan normals) are mostly inference.

### Cited Findings
- [Published, cited] (extraction) SuperLoc: pre-optimisation observability matrix C_w from scan geometry and normals, claim that Hessian methods are "post-optimization (too late)" and "suffer from numerical instability" in degenerate cases; the extraction notes "does not provide direct experimental comparisons with X-ICP or Zhang's method on alignment risk prediction". — [SuperLoc HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02901)
- [Published, cited] (preprint, secondary summary) LF-GICP: regularised-covariance GICP adds an isotropic information floor to the translation Hessian block so "the Hessian" can look well-conditioned while the raw map-normal field is degenerate; the proposed statistics are read "before regularization"; thresholds calibrated on KITTI 00 and GEODE Tunnel01 and frozen; separation margins thin. — [Pith synopsis](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) ICET: analysing reference-scan voxel distributions before the iterative registration is described as fundamentally pre-solve, in contrast to post-hoc error analysis in alternatives. — [ICET](https://navi.ion.org/content/71/2/navi.647)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) X-ICP and LP-ICP both work per correspondence after association, using map-frame normals (X-ICP) and Jacobian projections onto Hessian eigenvectors; X-ICP transforms matched correspondences back into the LiDAR frame to remove the effect of map size on rotational eigenvectors. LP-ICP adds point-to-line constraints and states the X-ICP formula is a special case, and X-ICP "does not utilize point-to-line localizability information". — [X-ICP](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.16335v4), [LP-ICP](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.02580v1)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Hatleskog and Alexis: Hessian eigenvalues "depend on the number of measurements, weights, the scale of the environment and sensor noise". — [arXiv 2410.10784](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10784v2)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) LiLi: static-correspondence Hessian analysis is limited because it ignores re-association; reports Hessian-based (Zhang) open-cylinder error 0.382 m versus 0.037 m for the perturbation-based detector and a tunnel round trip where Zhang's method failed. — [LiLi HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17145v2)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Field analysis (Tuna et al. 2024): the ill-conditioning "stems directly from the environment's lack of information" rather than algorithmic choices, which is the argument for detection mechanisms tied to environment geometry; the paper does not itself compare raw-scan and Hessian detection. — [Field analysis](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.11809v2)
- [Published, cited] DCReg: the Hessian-based detectors differ systematically among themselves on the same data (FCN over-detects, ME under-detects, diagonal-block misses coupled degeneracy), so even among post-solve detectors the verdict depends on how the Hessian is used. — [DCReg arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.06285)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) Zhang et al. 2026: it does not compare raw-scan geometry with Hessian detection, but shows that the verdict of a Hessian-based detector flips with body-frame origin (0.1-1.0% of frame pairs for binary labels, up to 69.5% of pose corrections differ), i.e. the detector is not a function of geometry alone. — [arXiv 2608.15532](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15532)
- [Published, cited] (extraction) GEODE: methods "fail to correctly identify the direction of degradation"; benchmark sequences include translation-only, rotation-only and combined degeneracy. — [GEODE](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04961v1)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Where a raw-scan T and the solver H_tt/N will differ: (1) edge/corner features: LOAM-style pipelines add point-to-line residuals, which contribute information in two directions not captured by plane normals; T from plane normals underestimates information there (LP-ICP's point-to-line terms are the published example); (2) robust weights (Huber/Cauchy/GNC) and correspondence rejection (distance gates, normal-compatibility gates such as X-ICP's cos(80 deg) filter): the Hessian is the sum over accepted, weighted pairs, whereas T is over all scan normals; points outside the map overlap, dynamic objects and unmatched returns inflate T; (3) map versus scan normals: the Hessian built from map-plane normals (LOAM, FAST-LIO, X-ICP) can contain geometry beyond the current field of view and different noise, while scan normals are noisier but are what the sensor sees; for large incidence angles scan normals are biased towards the line of sight, which biases T; (4) regularised or covariance-weighted solvers (GICP) add isotropic floors (LF-GICP's point); (5) IMU prior terms make the solved problem better conditioned than the geometry alone. Consequently T is a necessary-condition-style proxy ("is the scene geometry able to constrain translation?"), whereas H is a statement about the actual solved problem; neither dominates.
- [Inference] The added value of a pre-solve channel is therefore (a) timing (a pre-solve warning allows switching the initial guess or weighting before the optimiser runs; SuperLoc and Switch-SLAM's own switching design make that argument), (b) independence from solver-specific effects that can mask or fabricate degeneracy, and (c) a different failure mode of detection error, which supports fusion. The added value over Hessian detection has not been demonstrated head-to-head in the literature found; it would have to be shown by the project on labelled data (corridor, tunnel, stairs from GEODE/SubT-MRS) with a confusion-matrix comparison of T-based, H_tt/N-based and Schur-based verdicts on the same frames.
- [Inference] Because H_tt = N_w * T_w (T over accepted weighted normals), the two channels become identical when the same normals, weights and correspondences are used, so any measured difference is attributable to the factors in the first bullet. A cheap ablation: compute T three ways (all scan normals; matched normals; matched and weighted map normals) and compare each with the Hessian-based verdict.

### Gaps
- No direct head-to-head benchmark (same data, same labels) of pre-solve normals statistics versus post-solve Hessian statistics; the closest are claim-level comparisons in SuperLoc and LF-GICP.
- SuperLoc and LF-GICP full texts not read in detail (extraction/synopsis only); LF-GICP is a 2026 preprint with thin threshold margins and may change.
- No source found quantifying how robust weights or correspondence rejection change detection verdicts; that is inference only.
- Whether any of the cited detectors was validated on labelled ground truth of degeneracy (beyond synthetic analytic geometries in Zhang et al. 2026 and the benchmark GEODE) was not established.
