# Test the smallest eigenvalue, not uniformity

**Bottom line.** Keep Channel T, but stop testing whether the normals are uniform. Test whether the smallest eigenvalue λ1 of the orientation tensor T is above a requirement. The current statistic B = (15N/2)Σ(lᵢ − 1/3)² ~ χ²(5) is the published Bingham uniformity test ([García-Portugués & Verdebout](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00286)). It asks "is the scene perfectly isotropic?" and its non-centrality grows linearly in N, so at LiDAR-scale N it rejects every real scene, safe or degenerate. The defensible replacement has four parts, and only the first has a standard-statistics basis (the delta method for a simple eigenvalue). (1) A **one-sided lower confidence bound on l1**, with H0: λ1 ≤ τ (degenerate) and H1: λ1 > τ. It uses a cluster-robust standard error computed from per-patch scatter sums, never from raw point counts. (2) A **noise-floor null**. Normals fitted to noisy points put a floor of about s² under l1 even in a truly degenerate scene, so the floor must be estimated by running the production pipeline on a simulated degenerate copy of each scene. (3) A **three-state output** (constrained, degenerate, uncertain) feeding a **CUSUM on log l1** whose threshold is set by dependence-preserving resampling. (4) A **three-arm experiment** (Hessian-only, T-only, both) on geometry-defined labels, scored per segment with paired tests. Nearly all formulas in this report were derived or simulated by the research agents. Only the Bingham uniformity test, the Woodcock/Vollmer descriptors and the Bingham/Fisher–Bingham likelihood facts were checked against retrieved published sources. Several earlier project claims need correcting (Section 5), including that Channel T is only H_tt/N when normals, weights and correspondences are identical, which your Multi Floor log shows they are not.

## Why B tests the wrong hypothesis, in plain language and in formulas

Think of each LiDAR normal as an axis (n and −n are the same). T is the 3×3 "average of nnᵀ", with trace 1. A perfectly well-conditioned scene (three orthogonal planes, equal areas) gives T = I/3. Uniform normals over the sphere also give T ≈ I/3, which is why the Bingham test uses distance from I/3 as its signal. For p = 3, B = (15N/2)Σ(lᵢ − 1/3)² is the published uniformity statistic, with a χ²(5) null ([García-Portugués & Verdebout](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00286)) [Published, verified]. The test is based on E[UUᵀ] = I/p under uniformity, and the same source notes it has little power against alternatives that keep E[UUᵀ] near I/p.

Three consequences follow, and each is a derivation, not a published statement. [Derived/simulated] For any true T ≠ I/3, B is non-central χ²(5) with non-centrality (15N/2)Σ(λᵢ − 1/3)², so B/N → (15/2)Σ(λᵢ − 1/3)² > 0 and the test always rejects eventually. First, a perfectly safe anisotropic scene such as diag(0.5, 0.3, 0.2) gives B ≈ 350 at N = 1000 (report-writer arithmetic), against a 5% critical value of 11.07. A corridor-like diag(0.02, 0.49, 0.49) gives B ≈ 1100. Both reject by two orders of magnitude, so B cannot separate them. Second, B is symmetric in the eigenvalues: it cannot distinguish "one eigenvalue near zero" from "one eigenvalue large". Third, the normals of neighbouring points are strongly dependent, so the effective N is the number of independent patches, not the point count. The research agents' simulations found that using naive N makes a nominal 95% bound cover only 68–87% of the time when normals are clustered by patch.

The only regime where the uniformity null matters is "is the scene isotropic?". Under exact uniformity, √N(T̂ − I/3) converges to the traceless part of a 3×3 symmetric Gaussian matrix with off-diagonal standard deviation √(1/15) [Derived/simulated, consistent with the published χ²(5) via (15/2)·E tr(W²) = 5]. This gives l1 ≈ 1/3 − O(0.26/√N), a spread of about 0.008 at N = 1000. That is an isotropy diagnostic, nothing more. Keep B as a descriptive effect-size check and report it as such. One thing to fix in your notes: the project methodology document, as read by one research agent, writes B = 15N Σ(lᵢ − 1/3)². The published form has 15N/2, as in your task statement. The 11.07 critical value is only consistent with the 15N/2 form.

The statistics literature has no off-the-shelf test of H0: λ1 ≤ τ for an axial sample. The classical Watson and Bingham tests are uniformity or symmetry tests. Woodcock's shape/strength indices and Vollmer's P, G, R indices (R = 3·smallest eigenvalue) are descriptors, not tests ([Geological Magazine paper restating the formulas](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/25DAA8C00B6178AA81D0E177A7DC5C7B/S0016756822000747a.pdf/magnetic_shape_fabric_analysis_from_syntectonic_granites_a_study_based_on_the_eigenvalue_method.pdf)) [Published, verified via a downstream paper, not the primary]. In your ascending notation, R = 3·l1 is the descriptive form of the l1 criterion. The primary texts (Mardia & Jupp, Watson 1983, Anderson 1963, Fisher–Lewis–Embleton, Woodcock & Naylor) were not retrievable, so any statement about their exact tests is [Published, pointer only/unverified].

## The recommended tests, with formulas, hypotheses and recipes

### Test A: one-sided lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue

Plain version: if the smallest eigenvalue of your measured T is only just above the requirement, you cannot be sure the next scan, or the scene without a door frame, is constrained. A confidence bound turns "l1 = 0.06" into "we are 95% sure λ1 ≥ 0.04". The direction matters. Choose the null to be the state whose wrongful rejection is costlier, which for localisation safety is "degenerate". Then a rejection is positive certification of observability, and noisy or small-evidence frames default to the conservative mode.

Notation: nᵢ unit normals, T̂ = (1/N)ΣnᵢnᵢT, l1 ≤ l2 ≤ l3 with eigenvector v1, and yᵢ = (v1ᵀnᵢ)², so l1 = mean(yᵢ). [Derived/simulated] The matrix CLT and first-order perturbation give √N(l1 − λ1) → N(0, σ1²), with σ1² = Var(x1²) = E[x1⁴] − λ1², where x1 = v1ᵀn. Only boundedness of n is used, no normality. The plug-in variance is σ̂1² = mean(yᵢ²) − l1². In the small-λ1 regime x1 is roughly N(0, λ1), so σ1² ≈ 2λ1² and Var(log l1) ≈ (K1 − 1)/N, with K1 = mean(yᵢ²)/l1² the empirical kurtosis ratio (3 for a Gaussian, larger when outlier normals or clutter inflate the tail).

The tests, and the three-state decision, are:

```
Lower bound   L = l1 − z_{1−α} · se          (log form: L = l1 · exp(−z · sqrt((K1−1)/N_eff)))
Upper bound   U = l1 + z_{1−α} · se          (log form: U = l1 · exp(+z · sqrt((K1−1)/N_eff)))
Test A   H0: λ1 ≤ τ   vs  H1: λ1 > τ   reject (certify constrained) iff  L > τ_ok
Test B   H0: λ1 ≥ τ   vs  H1: λ1 < τ   reject (certify degenerate)  iff  U < τ_deg
Output   CONSTRAINED if L > τ_ok;  DEGENERATE if U < τ_deg;  else UNCERTAIN (keep the other channel / conservative mode)
```

The two-threshold form is a TOST-style (two one-sided tests) indifference zone, and it is the same as running both one-sided tests. TOST and Berger & Hsu's intersection-union framing were not retrieved ([Berger & Hsu 1996](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-11/issue-4/Bioequivalence-trials-intersection-union-tests-and-equivalence-confidence-sets/10.1214/ss/1032280304.pdf), [Lakens primer](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/)) [Published, pointer only/unverified]. The fail-safe logic has a published analogue in integrity monitoring: chi-square RAIM runs a fault test with a false-alert-controlled threshold, and separately demands that geometry is good enough before the system is declared available ([Joerger et al.](https://www.aoe.vt.edu/content/dam/aoe_vt_edu/people/faculty/joerger/publications/Fault_Detection_and_Exclusion_Using_Solution_Separation_and_Chi_Squared_RAIM.pdf)) [Published, verified via summary]. Mapping this to LiDAR is [Inference].

**The standard error must be cluster-robust.** [Derived/simulated] Partition the points into K spatial cells (voxels, scan-line segments or planar-segment labels), with n_c points and mean ȳ_c in cell c, and weights w_c = n_c/N. A batch-means/cluster-robust estimator is

```
se² = ( K / (K−1) ) · Σ_c w_c² · ( ȳ_c − l1 )²      N_eff ≈ K  (when cells absorb the correlation)
```

The agents described this estimator and its simulation, and the weighted form above is the report writer's write-out of it, so check it before coding. The simulation (clustered patch model, nominal 95% lower bound, 3000 repetitions) gave naive-N coverage of 0.77, 0.71, 0.68, 0.75 and 0.87 for K = 10, 30, 100, 300, 1000 patches, against 0.994, 0.990, 0.975, 0.970, 0.967 with the cluster-based standard error. With N in the thousands and only tens of patches, N_eff ≈ K. Under independent sampling the plain delta-method bound is valid or slightly conservative from N ≈ 50 upward (coverage 0.985 at N = 50 down to 0.950 at N = 10,000), so dependence, not N, is the binding constraint. K ≥ 20–30 cells is the working rule from the notes.

**Near-multiplicity.** The normal approximation needs λ1 well separated from λ2. Plane-only scenes (open ground, a single floor) have two small eigenvalues. Two remedies: bound the smooth sum l1 + l2, or use the fixed-direction test (row 4 of the table), which is a plain t-test on cell means of yᵢ = (uᵀnᵢ)² with no eigenproblem. [Derived/simulated] Report l2 − l1 against its standard error with every verdict, and treat the bound as unreliable when the gap is not several standard errors. A non-asymptotic cross-check, matrix Chernoff, is distribution-free but conservative. It is useful only to confirm that N_eff·λ1 is not of order one, and it was recalled from Tropp (2012), not retrieved [Published, pointer only/unverified].

**Cheap robustness companion.** [Derived/simulated] Since T̂ = Σ_c S_c (the cell scatter sums), leave-one-cell-out values l1^(−c) = λ_min(T̂ − S_c) cost O(1) each. The jackknife standard error SE_jack² = ((K−1)/K)Σ_c(l1^(−c) − mean)² and the worst case min_c l1^(−c) answer "would the constraint survive losing any one structure?". This catches the common failure where one door frame or pillar supplies all of l1. It is descriptive and carries no size guarantee.

**Percentile-t cluster bootstrap.** [Derived/simulated, proposal] Resample cells with replacement, recompute l1*, se*, form t* = (l1* − l1)/se*, and use the α quantile for the bound. The Fisher–Hall and Fisher–Hall–Jing–Wood pivotal bootstrap papers for directional data exist but were not readable, so whether they treat eigenvalue bounds is unknown ([Fisher et al., ResearchGate listing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/246432776_Improved_Pivotal_Methods_for_Constructing_Confidence_Regions_With_Directional_Data)) [Published, pointer only/unverified]. A bootstrap of a minimum eigenvalue is not guaranteed consistent when the eigenvalue is repeated, so work on the log scale and check separation.

### The noise floor: the statistic that manufactures its own information

Plain version: a degenerate corridor's normals are not exactly perpendicular to the corridor axis, because every normal is fitted from noisy points. That tilt leaks a little "x-component" into every normal, so l1 never reaches zero. Treated naively, the leak looks like genuine information along the corridor. The noise-floor note shows that this leak can be larger than any plausible threshold on information in metres, and it is the mechanism behind "noise-fitted normals manufacture precision".

[Derived/simulated] Let eᵢ be the tangent-plane error of normal i, with per-axis variance s². If all true normals are perpendicular to v, then E[T_vv] = s² − 4s⁴ (isotropic Gaussian) and E[l1] = s²(1 − 2/N), with N·T_vv/s² ~ χ²_N for independent homoscedastic errors. For heteroscedastic errors (variance sᵢ² varying with range and incidence), the Satterthwaite degrees of freedom are ν = (Σsᵢ²)²/Σsᵢ⁴. The key correlation result is Cov(xᵢ², xⱼ²) = 2Cov(xᵢ, xⱼ)² for zero-mean bivariate Gaussians, so correlation enters squared: N_eff = N²/Σᵢⱼρᵢⱼ². Robust weights that depend on the noisy normal or fit residual truncate tails and invalidate chi-square nulls, so only a bootstrap through the same weighting is safe. In a ray-cast VLP-16-like corridor (3.0 m × 2.5 m, N ≈ 28,500, σ_r = 0.02 m, PCA normals with k = 20), the noisy l1 was about 0.019 versus a noise-free value of 0.004–0.009, with a within-scan standard deviation of only 1.4% of the mean. Level misspecification, not sampling variance, dominates. The estimator also has a deterministic bias even at zero noise (4×10⁻³ to 9×10⁻³ depending on k and range limit) because PCA on ring-sampled planes is biased. Closed-form angular-error formulas (isotropic discs, Mitra–Nguyen-type bounds ([Mitra, Nguyen & Guibas](https://geometry.stanford.edu/lgl_2024/papers/mng-esnpcd-04/mng-esnpcd-04.pdf)) [Published, verified via summary]) under-predicted the simulated floor by about 2.5× for ring-structured neighbourhoods. Do not trust them; simulate the real pipeline.

The calibration recipes, in the order recommended:

**R1 (primary): parametric bootstrap through the production pipeline.** [Derived/simulated; the recipe is a proposal, not a published standard]
1. Choose v and state the null "all surfaces parallel to v". RANSAC-fit the dominant planes of each scene and refit them constrained parallel to v. Optionally add the real residual roughness.
2. Replay the real sampling geometry (ring, azimuth, dropouts). For each real return, set the range to the ray–plane intersection with the surrogate and add N(0, σ_r²) noise along the beam. Estimate σ_r from range residuals to fitted planes at small incidence, using the datasheet only as a sanity check.
3. Run the exact production pipeline (same k or radius, filters, robust weights, gating, T assembly) at least 200 times per scene type. Record mean μ0, standard deviation and 99th percentile q99, and report the noise-free l1 separately because it is part of the null.
4. Repeat with σ_r × {0.75, 1, 1.5} and k ± 5, and take the largest q99 as the conservative threshold.
5. Averaging over W scans lowers the standard deviation but not the floor, which does not average down. Do not shrink the floor by W.
6. Decision: "significantly informative along v" iff l1 > q99. Use floor-subtracted information, l1_excess = max(0, l1 − μ0), in any physical formula.

**R2 (analytic cross-check):** per-point sᵢ² = σ_n,i² Σ_m (v·u_im)²/λ_im with σ_n,i² = λ_i3/(k − 3) from the plane fit's k − 3 degrees of freedom, then Satterthwaite. [Derived/simulated] It under-predicted the VLP-16-like floor by about 2.5× at k = 20 and diverged at k = 10. Use it only as a sanity check, or for isotropic normal estimators.

**R3 (empirical split-conformal on real degenerate stretches):** take m non-overlapping windows known to be degenerate for v (surveyed straight corridor stretches), hold some out, and set the threshold at the ⌈(m+1)(0.99)⌉-th smallest calibration value. It needs m ≥ 100 for a non-trivial 99% threshold and block-exchangeability under autocorrelation. [Derived/simulated, validity under autocorrelation unchecked.] It includes real small structure, so it is an upper envelope (more conservative than R1).

**R4 (cross-fitting):** build normals twice for the same locations from independent noise (two scans of a static scene, or alternating-rank halves of each neighbourhood) and take l1 of (T_AB + T_ABᵀ)/2, with T_AB = (1/N)Σ nᵢᴬ(nᵢᴮ)ᵀ. [Derived/simulated] In simulation the cross-fit l1 was 9.5×10⁻³, 9.6×10⁻³, 9.5×10⁻³, 1.0×10⁻² at σ_r = 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.03 m, versus 1.0×10⁻², 1.2×10⁻², 1.9×10⁻², 4.1×10⁻² for the self-T over the same sigmas. The noise term cancels, but a deterministic bias (about 9.5×10⁻³ in that scene, not equal to the noise-free 6.1×10⁻³) remains, so a simulated baseline is still required.

**Validation before trusting any null.** On surveyed degenerate stretches, the fraction of windows with l1 > q99 should be about 1%, and mean(l1)/μ0 should be near 1. A ratio well above 1 means unmodelled noise (σ_r too small, motion distortion, or real structure); well below 1 means an over-smooth null. A dose-response check (add synthetic range noise to real data and compare the shift in l1 with the simulated curve) tests the null's shape [Inference].

**Physical threshold.** [Derived arithmetic] Translation information along unit v is I_v = N·T_vv/σ², so the Cramér–Rao bound is σ_v ≥ σ/√(N·T_vv) (the Fisher form implicit in [Censi's CRB](https://censi.science/research/robot-perception/crb-localization/) and Gelfand's Hessian with the rotation block dropped ([Gelfand et al.](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/stabicp/stabicp.pdf)) [Published, verified via summary]). A precision requirement σ_req therefore needs l1_excess ≥ σ²/(N·σ_req²); N = 28,000, σ = 0.02 m and σ_req = 0.05 m give 5.7×10⁻⁶. In the simulated corridor, the raw floor (0.019) would claim σ_v ≈ 0.9 mm for a truly degenerate scene, which shows why floor subtraction is mandatory before a metre-valued threshold means anything. The published literature supports the broader point that Hessian-based covariances are badly over-optimistic: Censi's closed form is "widely considered overoptimistic" ([Brossard et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.05722)) and CELLO-3D reports Censi's prediction off by KL divergence of order 10⁷–10⁸ versus about 27 for the learned model ([Landry et al.](https://norlab.ulaval.ca/pdf/Landry2019.pdf)) [Published, verified via summary; numbers to be re-checked]. The agents found no published inflation factor that targets the normal-estimation noise floor of the Hessian directly.

### What the Bingham family still contributes

[Derived from exponential-family theory; Bingham 1974 and Kent 1987 recalled, not retrieved] The Bingham density f(x) ∝ exp(Σκⱼ xⱼ²) is a regular exponential family whose sufficient statistic is T̂. The MLE axes are T̂'s eigenvectors and the MLE concentrations solve E_κ[xⱼ²] = lⱼ, a monotone bijection onto the ordered eigenvalue simplex. So a fitted concentration κ̂1 carries no information beyond (l1, l2, l3), a Bingham likelihood-ratio test is a function of the lⱼ that also needs the normalising constant, and the l1 decision needs none of that. The normalising constant has a one-dimensional contour-integral representation, with Kume–Wood saddlepoint approximations and an exact holonomic-gradient evaluation ([Sei & Kume](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.7973); [Kume & Wood 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/biomet/v92y2005i2p465-476.html)) [Published, verified]. In the strong-girdle limit a Laplace argument gives κ1 ≈ −1/(2·λ1) (λ1 = 0.01 gives κ1 ≈ −50) [Derived/simulated]. A Bingham fit is useful for goodness-of-fit and for generating parametric-bootstrap nulls, and the Bingham filter for tracking a dominant axis ([Kurz et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.8019)) is a state estimator, not a concentration tracker for a stream of normals [Published, verified]. The cost of an eventual Bingham MLE is an estimate only: a 3×3 eigendecomposition plus 5–20 Newton iterations on the constant, about 0.1–1 ms in compiled code. No timing was found, and the fit would buy nothing for the l1 decision [Inference].

The honest thesis framing is therefore an "orientation-tensor channel with a Bingham-family interpretation": the Bingham model names the distribution whose sufficient statistic T is, and the valid inference is on T's eigenvalues. Claiming that Bingham parameters add discriminating power over eigenvalues would be wrong.

For a secondary diagnostic, a Wald test of axial symmetry about v1 (is the girdle isotropic in its plane?) is Q = 2N‖d‖²/E[(1 − x1²)²] → χ²₂, with d = (B22 − B33, 2B23) in the plane perpendicular to v1 [Derived/simulated]. It tests symmetry, not degeneracy, and a corridor (λ2 ≠ λ3) rejects it correctly and irrelevantly.

## The switching layer: a CUSUM on log l1, calibrated against autocorrelation

The current plan runs a Bernoulli CUSUM on a binary verdict, which discards the margin: l_min = 0.14 and 0.01 look identical. Replace it with a one-sided downward CUSUM on a standardised continuous score. [Inference] Use yₜ = log l1,t (the statistic lives in (0, 1/3], is skewed, and the log makes shifts roughly additive). Standardise with frozen in-control mean and standard deviation from adequate-geometry sequences, pre-whiten with an AR(p) chosen by AIC/BIC on the same segment, optionally Winsorise at about ±3 so single bad frames cannot trip it, and run Sₜ = max(0, Sₜ₋₁ + (−zₜ) − k) with k = δ/2, where δ is the smallest standardised drop in log l1 that is operationally harmful (taken from pose-error data, not from the detector). Page's CUSUM is exactly optimal under Lorden's criterion for iid data and first-order asymptotically minimax ([Polunchenko & Tartakovsky](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.2938); [Moustakides](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.0741)) [Published, verified]. A restarting CUSUM suits a corridor that is entered and left repeatedly, whereas Shiryaev–Roberts has the edge after long quiet periods ([multi-cyclic analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.5002)) [Published, pointer only/unverified; the choice is [Inference]].

**Autocorrelation is the dominant failure mode.** [Derived/simulated, unit-variance Gaussian AR(1) input, k = 0.5, h = 5, 400 runs each] ARL0 was about 996 (φ = 0), 109 (φ = 0.5), 67 (φ = 0.8), and 913 after pre-whitening at φ = 0.8. At 10 Hz these are about 100 s, 11 s, 6.7 s, and 91 s between false alarms. Real frame statistics are neither Gaussian nor AR(1), so this illustrates mechanism, not your rates. Siegmund's closed form for the iid case, ARL = [exp(−2Δb) + 2Δb − 1]/(2Δ²) with b = h + 1.166, gave 938 against a Monte Carlo 996 ± 53 and is only a first guess for h. Set h by Monte Carlo on held-out in-control logs, resampled with a dependence-preserving bootstrap. The Politis–White automatic block-length rule gives the stationary-bootstrap block length ([Politis & White 2004](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Politis_White_2004.pdf)) [Published, verified]; a 2009 correction to its constants was not read, so use a maintained implementation. Report false alarms per hour with a bootstrap upper confidence limit, not only ARL0, because run lengths are roughly geometric (an ARL0 of 3000 frames still alarms within 300 frames about 10% of the time) [Inference]. Per-frame conformal thresholds control marginal exceedance, not the ARL0 of a sequential chart, and adaptive conformal inference ([Gibbs & Candès](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170)) needs online labels a deployed SLAM system lacks, so use it only for offline recalibration. Relatedly, non-exchangeable conformal prediction has a coverage bound that degrades with a weighted total-variation distance ([Barber et al.](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10433171)) [Published, verified via summary]. No published minimum-eigenvalue control chart for orientation tensors was found; the nearest are eigenvalue charts for covariance variability, content unread [Published, pointer only/unverified].

A matrix EWMA, T̄ₜ = (1 − a)T̄ₜ₋₁ + aTₜ, remains trace-1 positive semi-definite and supplies a smoothed l1 to read off each frame [Inference]. The CUSUM then supplies the decision, with the signed margin zₜ = (log L − log τ)/sₜ if you want hysteresis around the three-state output.

## Experimental protocol: does Channel T add value over the Hessian/Schur channel?

The literature gives no precedent for this comparison. No paper was found that benchmarks a pre-solve normal statistic against the post-solve Hessian verdict on the same data with labels. SuperLoc claims earlier warnings but its extraction notes no direct detection comparison against X-ICP or Zhang ([SuperLoc](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02901)), LF-GICP argues regularisation can mask degeneracy in the Hessian ([LF-GICP synopsis](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)), and LiLi reports a Hessian method failing where its own perturbation-based detector succeeds ([LiLi](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.17145v2)) [Published, verified via summary or secondary synopsis]. The three-arm design below is therefore a contribution, not a replication.

**Arms (decided before looking at test data).** Arm H: Hessian/Schur-only detector (your DCReg-style S_RR, S_tt condition numbers or the D1 test). Arm T: Channel T alone, using the calibrated Test A/B plus floor subtraction and the CUSUM layer. Arm H+T: the pre-registered fusion rule (recommended: degenerate if either arm says degenerate, plus a score-level logistic model as a secondary analysis). Each arm gets its own threshold, calibrated on calibration sequences to the same false-alarm rate per hour, so that sensitivity is compared at matched false-alarm rate, not at each arm's default setting.

**Ground truth that no detector sees.** In Gazebo, label from the known mesh: compute the true T (and the true Hessian) from ground-truth surface normals under the same ray-cast sampling, with the true pose, and label a frame degenerate along v from that quantity. Keep the labelling threshold distinct from every detector threshold and sweep it as a sensitivity analysis (the project's own earlier validation had circular labels). Build corridor, room and tunnel worlds, and add graded variants (door frames, pillars, partial end walls, range limit, σ_r) so that λ1(truth) spans a continuum and you can plot a dose-response curve for each arm. On SubT-MRS Multi Floor and Long Corridor, geometry ground truth is not available from the sensor alone, so use outcome-based labels: a segment is degenerate along v if LiDAR-only pose error along v exceeds a tolerance relative to the reference trajectory. Whether SubT-MRS provides the reference trajectory quality needed for this was not checked [Published, pointer only/unverified]. Treat real-data labels as noisier than Gazebo labels and say so.

**Unit of analysis and splits.** Segments and sequences, not frames. At 10 Hz, 1000 frames is 100 s and may be a few independent scenes. The effective sample size for a stationary series is n(1 − φ)/(1 + φ) for AR(1) (about 53 for n = 1000 at φ = 0.9) [Inference/background, unverified source]. Calibrate means, variances, AR coefficients, h and floors on calibration sequences, freeze, and test on sequences from other environments. A random frame-level split leaks information through autocorrelation.

**Primary and secondary analyses.**
1. *Primary:* paired comparison of H+T against H at the segment level, run separately on truly degenerate segments (sensitivity) and truly well-conditioned segments (specificity). Use McNemar's test with the continuity-corrected χ² = (|b − c| − 1)²/(b + c), or the exact binomial on discordant pairs (McNemar [Published, pointer only/unverified]). Pair at segment level so adjacent-frame dependence does not inflate significance. Report the difference in rates with a cluster (sequence) bootstrap interval.
2. *Sample size.* [Derived by report writer, standard normal-approximation arithmetic] To detect a discordant split of 75:25 in favour of H+T at α = 0.05, power 0.8, you need about 29 discordant segments (n ≈ [(1.96·0.5 + 0.84·0.433)/0.25]²). The number of total segments needed depends on the discordance rate and must be planned from a pilot.
3. *Ranking comparison:* AUC or PR curves on the continuous scores (Hessian condition number or Schur eigenvalue ratio versus l1 excess), with a sequence-bootstrap interval on the AUC difference. State the prevalence of degenerate frames.
4. *Incremental information:* fit a logistic model of the label on Arm H's features, then test whether adding Channel T features reduces deviance, with cluster-robust standard errors. A significant gain proves added value in the strict sense; a null result supports the earlier "T carries no extra information" claim in that data.
5. *Timing:* detection delay (first alarm minus labelled onset, with censoring, summarised by Kaplan–Meier) and recovery delay. The pre-solve argument is that T can warn before the optimiser runs. That claim is made by SuperLoc and the Switch-SLAM design, not shown head-to-head [Inference].
6. *Mechanism ablation (the key diagnostic of T versus H_tt/N):* compute T three ways and compare each against the Hessian verdict: all scan normals; matched normals only; matched and weighted map normals. The agents' reasoning is that the two channels are identical when normals, weights and correspondences match, so any measured gap is attributable to edge residuals, robust weights and rejection gates, map versus scan normals, regularisation, or IMU priors [Inference]. This ablation tells you which of those mechanisms drives the gap on your Multi Floor log.
7. *Downstream:* ATE/RPE and NEES under switching with each arm, plus forced-mode ablations.

**Falsification criteria, stated in advance.** Channel T has no demonstrated added value if (a) H+T does not exceed H in sensitivity at matched false-alarm rate with an interval excluding zero, or (b) its logistic increment is not significant after accounting for dependence, or (c) its pre-solve timing advantage is within the Hessian arm's delay distribution. State the result either way; a negative result on real data is still a defensible thesis finding.

**What must be measured on your own data.**
- The range noise σ_r, from residuals to fitted planes at small incidence, and the normal-estimation settings (k or radius).
- The floor μ0, q99 and the noise-free bias for each scene type and range profile.
- The cell definition and the number of independent patches K per scene, plus the design effect (the research agents saw about 3.3 for k = 20 in one simulated scan, which is not a property of your sensor).
- The empirical kurtosis K1 of x1 and the gap l2 − l1 against its standard error.
- The autocorrelation function of log l1, its marginal distribution in adequate scenes, and the AR order.
- The harmful δ (the drop in log l1 that precedes real pose error), from pose-error data.
- The three-way T versus H_tt/N relationship described above, and how l1 depends on N and on scan pattern (small-N bias).
- The false-alarm rate per hour on held-out in-control sequences. To estimate an ARL0 of about 3000 frames to ±20% needs roughly 25 independent in-control run lengths, about 75,000 frames (about 2 hours at 10 Hz) if dependence is handled by resampling. Demonstrating a rate below 10⁻³ with zero observed alarms needs about 3000 independent frames [Inference/background, from the zero-event 3/n rule, unverified source].

## Novelty statement and where it stops

To our knowledge, in the sources searched (web search plus fetched pages through 2026-10-06, without a Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore or Semantic Scholar keyword search with full result paging), we found no LiDAR registration or SLAM paper that (a) treats the scan's surface normals as an axial sample summarised by the orientation tensor T, (b) performs one-sided statistical inference on its smallest eigenvalue, a confidence bound or hypothesis test with a stated size and a cluster-robust variance that accounts for the effective number of independent patches, (c) calibrates the detection threshold against a null distribution for the statistic, including the noise floor that normal estimation puts under a truly degenerate scene, produced by running the production pipeline on a simulated degenerate copy of the scene, and (d) tests the added value of that channel over a Hessian/Schur detector in a controlled three-arm design with geometry-defined labels. The agents found no paper that calibrates any degeneracy threshold against a null distribution of the statistic (for example a bootstrap or asymptotic null of λ_min) (a survey conclusion across the Zhang 2016, Hinduja, X-ICP, LP-ICP, SuperLoc, DCReg, GenZ-ICP, LF-GICP and Switch-SLAM threshold provenances listed in the notes, most read via extractions). The claim is a negative search result bounded by the queries run, and it must not be written as "first".

The claim must be worded around three published neighbours so reviewers do not read it as overreach. First, LF-GICP's lambda_min(M)/tr(M) is the same eigenvalue ratio with thresholds frozen from two 500-frame sequences and no inference; the reviewer-style reading of its separation margins is "thin" ([synopsis](https://pith.science/paper/2608.19522)). Second, Hatleskog & Alexis propagate point and normal noise into the Hessian and report the probability that signal exceeds noise by a factor of ten ([arXiv 2410.10784](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.10784v2)), so noise-awareness is not new; what differs is a sampling-inference bound on T's smallest eigenvalue and a pipeline-bootstrap noise null in place of an analytic propagation with a chosen factor. Their paper was read through an extraction, so confirm that distinction against the full text. Third, Straub et al. use a Bingham distribution approximated by a von Mises–Fisher mixture on surfel normals in a direction-aware SLAM, arguing that diverse plane orientations constrain the point-to-plane cost ([arXiv 1709.05774](https://arxiv.org/html/1709.05774)), and a CMU course report explores Bingham mixtures for normal segmentation with an "inconclusive" result ([Madaan et al.](https://ratneshmadaan.github.io/files/slam_bingham_course_project.pdf)). Neither is a degeneracy test. A Bingham-based novelty claim is not supportable because the Bingham fit adds nothing over T's eigenvalues; claim the inference and calibration on T. Also keep in mind the two other post-solve alternatives that bypass Hessian static-correspondence assumptions, LiLi and the equivariant metric of Zhang et al. (arXiv 2608.15532, a single recent preprint), both read only through extractions ([Zhang et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.15532)). The latter shows that Hessian verdicts depend on the body-frame origin, which supports T's origin-independence as a selling point [Inference]. Searches for Kent, Watson, "orientation tensor", "structure tensor", "Gauss map" and "extended Gaussian image" with LiDAR degeneracy found nothing relevant, and the Horn extended-Gaussian-image literature and obscure theses cannot be ruled out.

## Conclusion

The practical result is a reordering of what is claimed. The Bingham framing earns no statistical power of its own, because every Bingham quantity is a monotone function of T's eigenvalues. The defensible contribution is the inference layered on those eigenvalues: a lower confidence bound whose variance counts patches, not points, a threshold set against the floor that normal estimation itself creates, and a switching rule whose false-alarm rate was measured under autocorrelation. The most surprising number in the notes is the scale of the floor, roughly 0.02 in a simulated corridor, against a physically meaningful threshold near 10⁻⁵. A raw l1 can only certify "informative" conservatively and can never resolve a small true T_vv.

The thesis question also changes character. "Is T equal to H_tt/N?" has a trivial answer under identical inputs and an empirical one on your log, where the two disagree in median smallest eigenvalue by a factor of about 2.4. Whether that disagreement is useful signal or noise is decided by the three-way ablation and the three-arm experiment, with a pre-stated falsification rule. A result in either direction is publishable, but only if the segment-level pairing, the matched false-alarm rates and the geometry-defined labels are in place before the real-data runs.
