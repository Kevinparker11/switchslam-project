# Sequential change detection and calibration for a per-frame orientation-tensor degeneracy statistic

Scope: turn a per-frame statistic derived from the 3x3 orientation tensor T = (1/N) sum n_i n_i^T (eigenvalues l1<=l2<=l3, trace 1) into a calibrated detector with controlled false-alarm rate (ARL0), for a LiDAR-only vs LiDAR+vision switch at 10 Hz.

- **[Published, pointer only]** = source identified by search (title/URL seen) but its content was NOT retrieved (paywall, robots.txt, 403 or 429). Do not treat its specific claims as verified.
- **[Own computation]** = I ran a simulation or arithmetic in this session (script reproduces in seconds; details given).
- **[Inference]** = my reasoning, not a published statement.
- **[Background, unverified]** = standard textbook result I believe is correct but could not retrieve a source for this session. The report writer should either cite from the textbook directly or soften.

Project context read (via project_search): the current plan uses a two-sided Bernoulli CUSUM on the D1 binary verdict (ARL0 about 3019 in a synthetic run, a and b to be measured on labelled data), and the Channel T rule is "degenerate if l_min < 0.15" with 0.15 an unvalidated empirical placeholder. The project also contains a derived (not yet implemented or validated) Bingham-uniformity test B = 15 N sum (l_i - 1/3)^2 ~ chi2_5 with 95% critical value 11.07 (attributed to Mardia & Jupp), and plans Wilson CIs with n >= 1068, geometry-defined ground truth, and held-out threshold sweeps. ## 1. CUSUM / EWMA / Shiryaev-Roberts / GLR for a continuous statistic; choosing k and h; ARL0 formulas; robust CUSUM; CUSUM under autocorrelation

### Takeaway
Replace the binary-flag CUSUM by a one-sided Gaussian-type CUSUM on a standardised continuous score (for example z_t = (log l_min,t - mu0)/sigma0 after pre-whitening), with k = delta/2 for the smallest shift delta worth catching and h set by Monte Carlo on held-out, dependence-preserving resamples, using Siegmund's formula only as a first guess. My own simulation shows a CUSUM tuned for iid data has its ARL0 fall from about 940 to about 109 (phi=0.5) and about 67 (phi=0.8) at lag-1 autocorrelation, and that AR(1) pre-whitening restores about 913, so ignoring autocorrelation is the dominant failure mode here.

### Cited Findings
- Page's CUSUM recursion is W_n = max(0, W_{n-1} + log Lambda_n) with Lambda_n the likelihood ratio; it is exactly optimal under Lorden's criterion for iid data (all gamma > 1) and first-order asymptotically minimax as ARL-to-false-alarm gamma -> infinity. — [Polunchenko & Tartakovsky, State-of-the-Art in Sequential Change-Point Detection](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.2938) [Published, retrieved]
- Shiryaev-Roberts (SR): R_n = (1 + R_{n-1}) Lambda_n, R_0 = 0, stop when R_n > A; it is the limit of Shiryaev's Bayesian procedure with geometric prior parameter p -> 0. Pollak's criterion J_P(T) = sup_{nu>=0} E_nu[T - nu | T > nu] is not solved in general; SR-r (start at R_0 = r > 0) is third-order asymptotically optimal under Pollak's criterion for suitable r = o(gamma). — [Polunchenko & Tartakovsky](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.2938) [Published, retrieved]
- Lorden's criterion is the worst-case delay ess sup E_nu[(T - nu)^+ | F_nu] over change points nu. ARL to false alarm = E_inf[T]. First-order delay relation: ADD ~ (1/I)[log(gamma zeta) + kappa - C_inf], with I the Kullback-Leibler number between post- and pre-change laws. — [Polunchenko & Tartakovsky](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1109.2938) [Published, retrieved]
- Moustakides (Ann. Statist. 14(4), 1986, "Optimal stopping times for detecting changes in distributions") proves CUSUM optimal under Lorden's criterion; Moustakides' later paper states that solving his optimisation problem for Lorden's criterion with iid observations "gives rise to the CUSUM test proposed by Page (1954)", and that Pollak (1985) proposed SR as candidate optimum for his criterion (asymptotic optimality shown), with Mei (2006) on non-asymptotic aspects. That later paper assumes a known post-change drift and does not address unknown post-change parameters, GLR, or nonparametric detection. — [Moustakides, Decision theoretic optimality of the CUSUM (arXiv 0804.0741)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0804.0741) [Published, retrieved]; 1986 paper itself at [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-statistics/volume-14/issue-4/Optimal-Stopping-Times-for-Detecting-Changes-in-Distributions/10.1214/aos/1176350164.full) [pointer only: page blocked]
- Tabular CUSUM: C_t^+ = max[0, (x_t - k) + C_{t-1}^+]; C_t^- = min[0, (x_t - k) + C_{t-1}^-]; reference value k is the midpoint between the in-control and out-of-control means, k = (mu1 + mu0)/2; limits h obtained from nomograms for specified ARL0/ARL1. — [Maghsoodloo CUSUM notes](https://www.eng.auburn.edu/~maghssa/INSY7330/Cusum-Control-Charts-M2013-Maghsoodloo.pdf) [Published, retrieved]. Same source cites Siegmund (1985) approximation with b = A + 1.166, A = h/sigma, but the formula as extracted by the fetch tool was garbled (see next bullet).
- Siegmund's ARL approximation, one-sided CUSUM on unit-variance observations, with Delta = drift of the increments (mu - k; in control Delta = -k) and b = h + 1.166: ARL = [exp(-2 Delta b) + 2 Delta b - 1] / (2 Delta^2) for Delta < 0 (in control), and ARL = [exp(2 Delta b) - 2 Delta b - 1] / (2 Delta^2) for Delta > 0. The 1.166 constant is the Siegmund continuous-to-discrete overshoot correction for the Gaussian case. The retrieved summary printed the formula as "[2b Delta - 1 - exp(-2b Delta)]/Delta^2", which is not the same expression, so I verified numerically: [Own computation] k=0.5, h=5, sigma=1 gives 938.2 from the formula against a Monte Carlo ARL0 of 996 +/- 53 (400 runs, one-sided, iid). The standard two-sided value for k=0.5, h=5 is about 465, which is consistent with two one-sided charts. — formula [Background, unverified for exact printed form; Siegmund 1985 *Sequential Analysis*, Montgomery *Intro. to Statistical Quality Control*], numerical check [Own computation]
- Basseville & Nikiforov's text treats CUSUM via the stopping rule g_k = max(0, g_{k-1} + s_k) > h, the GLR variants for unknown change magnitude, and ARL computations via Wald/Page integral equations. I could not retrieve the book this session. — [Published, pointer only] (Basseville & Nikiforov 1993, *Detection of Abrupt Changes: Theory and Application*, Prentice Hall)
- CUSUM charts for autocorrelated processes: the literature includes (a) charting the residuals of a fitted time-series model (AR(1) etc.) and (b) adjusting limits for the raw observations; Lu & Reynolds "CUSUM charts for monitoring an autocorrelated process" (J. Quality Technology 2001) and a Markov-chain ARL approach for AR(1) CUSUM (Springer chapter 10.1007/978-3-7908-2674-6_9), plus a distribution-free tabular CUSUM for autocorrelated data. I only saw titles; content not retrieved. — [CUSUM charts for monitoring an autocorrelated process](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/285907174_CUSUM_charts_for_monitoring_an_autocorrelated_process); [Markov approach to ARL of CUSUM for AR(1)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-7908-2674-6_9); [distribution-free tabular CUSUM for autocorrelated data](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/228888406_A_distribution-free_tabular_CUSUM_chart_for_autocorrelated_data) [Published, pointer only]
- For an AR(1) observation model the CUSUM change-point problem has an explicit Kullback-Leibler information I = (1/2)(lambda_0 - lambda_inf)^2/(1 - lambda_0^2) + ((1 - lambda_inf)^2/2)[mu_0/(1 - lambda_0) - mu_inf/(1 - lambda_inf)]^2 (as extracted; lambda = AR coefficient); both CUSUM and SR minimise SADD to within O(1); ARL and SADD evaluated by renewal equations or Monte Carlo. — [Comparative performance analysis of CUSUM, arXiv 1706.00824](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.00824) [Published, retrieved, formula from a model-written summary: verify before quoting]

### Inferences
- [Own computation] Simulation of the one-sided Gaussian CUSUM, k = 0.5, h = 5, unit-variance stationary AR(1) input, 400 runs each (cap 20,000 frames): ARL0 about 996 (phi=0, SE 53), about 109 (phi=0.5, SE 5), about 67 (phi=0.8, SE 3). With input pre-whitened using the true phi (z_t = (x_t - phi x_{t-1})/sqrt(1 - phi^2)) at phi=0.8: about 913 (SE 50). At 10 Hz these are about 100 s, 11 s, 6.7 s between false alarms. Positive autocorrelation shortens ARL0 by an order of magnitude; the effect is on ARL0, not just on a "slightly off" threshold. Script: scratchpad/sim.py; not a substitute for simulation on the user's own residuals, because real frame statistics are non-Gaussian and non-AR(1).
- [Inference] Recommended per-frame statistic, in order of preference: (i) y_t = log(l_min,t) or logit-type transform, because l_min lives in (0, 1/3] and is skewed with a hard lower boundary; the log stretches the region of interest (small l_min) and makes shifts closer to additive; (ii) standardise using in-control mean/variance estimated on a held-out "adequate geometry" segment; (iii) AR(p) pre-whiten, with p chosen by AIC/BIC on that segment; (iv) feed the standardised residual into a one-sided CUSUM downward (degeneracy = l_min going down): S_t = max(0, S_{t-1} + (-z_t) - k).
- [Inference] Choice of k: k = delta/2 where delta is the smallest standardised drop of log l_min that is operationally harmful (derived from geometry/pose-error data, not from the detector). This is the likelihood-ratio-optimal reference value for a Gaussian mean shift of size delta [Background, consistent with the "midpoint between in-control and out-of-control means" statement in the Maghsoodloo notes above]. If delta is uncertain (a range of degeneracy severities), use a two-CUSUM (fast/slow) bank, or a GLR/mixture CUSUM, or SR with a mixture over delta; the cost is a modest delay penalty and a more complex ARL0 calibration.
- [Inference] Choice of h: do not rely on the Siegmund formula for the deployed chart. Use it only to bracket h, then find h by Monte Carlo such that the empirical false-alarm rate on held-out adequate-geometry logs (resampled with a dependence-preserving bootstrap, see Section 3) equals the target. With a cap on the run: report the false alarm rate per hour, not only ARL0, since the ARL0 distribution is approximately geometric (SD about equal to mean), so an "ARL0 of 3000" still produces alarms within 300 frames about 10% of the time [Inference from geometric-tail heuristic: P(T<=m) ~ 1-exp(-m/ARL0)].
- [Inference] EWMA vs CUSUM: for small to moderate sustained shifts the two perform similarly when tuned to the same shift [Background, unverified]; CUSUM has the minimax optimality property (Moustakides) whereas EWMA has the practical advantage that its state is an interpretable smoothed estimate (here: smoothed log l_min with a confidence band). A hybrid is sensible: a matrix-EWMA of T supplies the estimate and the CUSUM supplies the decision.
- [Inference] SR vs CUSUM: SR has better average delay for far-away change times after long quiet periods (Pollak criterion); for a SLAM system where degenerate corridors occur repeatedly and switching back to "adequate" also matters, the multi-cyclic setting favours a restarting CUSUM (cheap, simple, state resets to 0) [see Polunchenko & Tartakovsky multi-cyclic work: [Efficient performance evaluation of the generalized SR procedure in a multi-cyclic setup, arXiv 1312.5002](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1312.5002), pointer only].
- [Inference] Binary verdict vs continuous score: a Bernoulli CUSUM discards the magnitude of the margin; two frames at l_min = 0.14 and 0.01 look identical. The Bernoulli CUSUM remains a valid fallback (distribution-free given a fixed verdict) but its ARL0 depends on the verdict's in-control flag rate p0 and inherits temporal correlation of the flags, so the same autocorrelation collapse applies; a "runs" or Markov-dependent Bernoulli CUSUM would be needed. I could not retrieve a source for the Markov-Bernoulli variant.
- [Inference] Robust / nonparametric options: (a) rank-based or sign-based CUSUM on the standardised score (distribution-free ARL0 only under iid, which pre-whitening does not guarantee); (b) Winsorise z_t at +/-c (about 3) before accumulation (Huber-type score ψ) to prevent single outlier frames (bad normal estimates, moving objects) from tripping the chart; (c) cap the per-frame increment. Published formulas for Huber-type CUSUM were not retrieved; present as design suggestions to be validated by simulation.

## 2. Multivariate / matrix monitoring; minimum-eigenvalue chart; Bingham filter

### Takeaway
There is established SPC literature on charting covariance matrices and their eigenvalues (generalized variance |S| charts, eigenvalue-based charts), but I found no published "minimum eigenvalue of an orientation tensor" chart, so a monitor of l_min of T is a project-specific construction whose null must be calibrated empirically. The Bingham filter (Kurz et al.) gives recursive Bayesian estimation of a directional state with explicit concentration matrix Z, but it is a state estimator with closed-form multiplicative updates for measuring a direction, not a conjugate recursive estimator of the Bingham parameter from a stream of normals; a matrix EWMA of T with block-bootstrap bands is the simpler fit for a smoothed concentration estimate.

### Cited Findings
- Bingham density on the unit hypersphere: f(x) = (1/F) exp(x^T M Z M^T x), M orthogonal (orientation), Z = diag(z_1 <= ... <= z_{d-1} <= 0) with last entry 0 (concentration), antipodally symmetric f(x) = f(-x); normalisation F = |S_{d-1}| 1F1(1/2, d/2, Z) with a hypergeometric function of matrix argument. — [Kurz, Gilitschenski, Julier, Hanebeck, Recursive estimation of orientation based on the Bingham distribution (arXiv 1304.8019)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.8019) [Published, retrieved]
- In that filter, prediction composes the state with system noise; since Bingham distributions are not closed under composition, they re-approximate by covariance-matrix matching; the update is Bayes' rule by density multiplication, and Bingham densities are closed under multiplication with renormalisation. For small uncertainty the Bingham resembles a Gaussian; for large uncertainty the Gaussian approximation is "quite poor". — [arXiv 1304.8019](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1304.8019) [Published, retrieved]
- Journal version for 3-D orientation (quaternions q and -q identical, handled by the Bingham on S^3): the concentration is represented by Z; covariance matrices map bijectively to parameters; the 2-D case reduces to Bessel functions; higher dimensions use a saddle-point approximation for the normaliser; reported non-optimised MATLAB cost on a laptop of about 8 ms (2-D) and 13 ms (4-D) per prediction-update cycle; limitations: more expensive than a Kalman filter, requires numerically solving nonlinear equations for parameters. — [Kurz et al., Recursive Bingham Filter for Directional Estimation Involving 180 Degree Symmetry, J. Adv. Inf. Fusion 2014](https://tisl.cs.utoronto.ca/publication/201412-jaif-bingham_filter/201412-jaif-bingham_filter.pdf) [Published, retrieved]
- Eigenvalue-based monitoring of process variability exists in the SPC literature: "Monitoring multivariate process variability via eigenvalues" (Computers & Industrial Engineering, 2017) [pointer only: page blocked], and "An optimal control chart for finite matrix sequences at some unknown change point" ([PMC9041645](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9041645), pointer only: rate-limited). I did not read either, so I cannot state their statistics or optimality claims.
- Project-internal: Bingham uniformity test B = 15 N sum_i (l_i - 1/3)^2 ~ chi2_5 under isotropic null, critical 11.07 at 5% (source: project doc, citing Mardia & Jupp). — [project doc SwitchSLAM_Methodology_Full_Corrected.md] [project document, not independently verified]

### Inferences
- [Inference] Density multiplication in the Bingham filter works because f ∝ exp(x^T C x) with C = M Z M^T: the product of two such densities is exp(x^T (C_1 + C_2) x), so the posterior parameter matrix is the sum C_1 + C_2 (follows directly from the retrieved density form). This is the update for a state x observed through a Bingham-shaped likelihood. It is not the likelihood of N iid normals given an unknown C (that likelihood contains F(C)^(-N), not of the same exponential-quadratic form). So the Bingham filter does not directly "track the concentration of the normal distribution"; it would track a latent dominant direction/orientation with uncertainty.
- [Inference] If the desired output is "smoothed orientation-concentration estimate with uncertainty", the cheaper and better-matched estimator is a matrix EWMA: T_bar_t = (1 - a) T_bar_{t-1} + a T_t (a convex combination of trace-1 PSD matrices stays trace-1 PSD), with l_min(T_bar_t) read off each frame; its uncertainty from a block bootstrap over frames and spatial blocks inside frames. The Bingham filter becomes worthwhile only if there is a dynamic model for the dominant-direction state (e.g., corridor axis in the world frame) and you want multi-hypothesis axis tracking with antipodal symmetry; cost ~8-13 ms in MATLAB is large against a 100 ms frame budget but feasible for 3-D axial data in C++ [cost numbers only from the paper's non-optimised MATLAB].
- [Inference] MEWMA / Hotelling-type charts monitor means of vector statistics; T has 5 free parameters (symmetric 3x3, trace 1). One option: monitor vech(T) - T0 with Hotelling/MEWMA using a robust, pre-whitened covariance Sigma_0 estimated from adequate-geometry frames; this detects any change in geometry, not only degeneracy. A directional chart (CUSUM on the projected statistic log l_min, or on the projection along the weak eigenvector) has more power for the specific one-sided hypothesis of interest. [Background, unverified: Hotelling T^2 and MEWMA formulas from Montgomery; not retrieved.]
- [Inference] Smallest-eigenvalue statistics have a non-standard null: l_min is a non-smooth function of T (bias at eigenvalue crossings, boundary at 0), the sampling law of l_min given N correlated normals does not follow Gaussian-theory limits, and the chi-square uniformity test with N in the formula will have massive power at LiDAR-scale N (thousands of normals) against even trivial departures from isotropy — essentially every real scene rejects isotropy. Therefore the isotropy null (chi2_5) answers "is the scene perfectly isotropic?" not "is the scene degenerate?" and should not be used as the detector's null; it can serve at most as an effect-size sanity check. Normals from neighbouring points are strongly dependent, so an "effective N" (number of independent surface patches) should replace N in any test statistic [Inference; magnitude must be measured].
- [Inference] A pragmatic matrix-chart design: per frame compute l_min and its bootstrap SE (resample spatial blocks, e.g., voxel/cell clusters, not individual normals). Use inverse-variance weighting in the CUSUM (frames with large SE contribute less) or declare "unknown" when the SE is large (see Section 4).

### Gaps
- No published minimum-eigenvalue control chart for orientation tensors (or for trace-normalised scatter matrices) was found in my searches; the closest are eigenvalue charts for covariance variability, content unread.
- Montgomery's generalized variance chart (|S| with control limits from the distribution of |S|), Hotelling T^2 and MEWMA (Lowry et al. 1992) not retrieved; formulas stated from memory only if used.
- Asymptotic distribution of eigenvalues of the second-moment matrix of unit vectors (Bingham 1974; Mardia & Jupp 2000, *Directional Statistics*) not retrieved; so no analytic null for l_min is given here. The project doc's chi2_5 test is the only analytic result and is for the isotropy null.
- No published treatment of Bingham filtering of a stream of surface-normal scatter matrices (as opposed to orientation state tracking) was found.

## 3. Empirical null calibration under temporal dependence: conformal, block/stationary bootstrap, EVT, binomial intervals, sample size

### Takeaway
Calibrate the threshold on labelled adequate-geometry frames with a dependence-aware procedure: choose the stationary-bootstrap block length by Politis-White, obtain thresholds from the bootstrap/empirical quantile with an honest effective sample size, and report Wilson (or Clopper-Pearson) intervals on rates computed after accounting for dependence. Plain split conformal has approximate validity only under weak (beta-mixing) dependence; adaptive conformal inference (ACI) gives a long-run miscoverage guarantee regardless of dependence, which is the appropriate guarantee for an alarm rate.

### Cited Findings
- Stationary bootstrap (Politis & Romano 1994, JASA 89): resample blocks with geometric-distributed length of mean b, wrapping around the series, so the bootstrap series is stationary. Optimal expected block length: b_opt^SB = (2 G^2 / D_SB)^(1/3) N^(1/3), with G = sum_{k=-inf..inf} k R_k (R_k autocovariance at lag k) and D_SB = 4 g^2(0) + (2/pi) integral_{-pi}^{pi} (1 + cos w) g^2(w) dw (g = spectral density). Practical rule: inspect the correlogram, pick the smallest m after which R_k is negligible, and take M = 2m for the flat-top lag window. — [Politis & White 2004, Automatic block-length selection for the dependent bootstrap](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Politis_White_2004.pdf) [Published, retrieved]; correction: [Patton, Politis & White 2009](https://public.econ.duke.edu/~ap172/Patton_Politis_White_2009.pdf) [pointer only, correction to the formula's constants; check before implementing]
- Split conformal under weak dependence: for stationary beta-mixing processes the coverage guarantee degrades to P[Y in C] >= 1 - alpha - (eps_cal + delta_cal + eps_train), slack terms bounding concentration error and training/test dependence; "for stationary, weakly dependent processes, standard SCP provides approximately valid coverage." — [Conformal prediction for time series, review/benchmark, arXiv 2511.13608](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.13608) [Published, retrieved as model summary; verify the slack-term definitions in the paper]
- Non-exchangeable conformal (Barber, Candes, Ramdas, Tibshirani, Ann. Statist. 2023): with weights w_i and normalised weights w~_i = w_i/(w_1+...+w_n+1), w~_{n+1} = 1/(w_1+...+w_n+1), the prediction set uses the weighted quantile Q_{1-alpha}(sum_i w~_i delta_{R_i}) and coverage satisfies P{Y_{n+1} in C} >= 1 - alpha - sum_i w~_i d_TV(R(Z), R(Z^i)), the sum of weighted total-variation distances between residual vectors after swapping point i with the test point. Weights should reflect trust (e.g., geometric decay w_i = rho^(n+1-i) for drift). — [Barber et al., Conformal prediction beyond exchangeability (NSF PAR)](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10433171) [Published, retrieved]
- Adaptive conformal inference (Gibbs & Candes 2021): online update alpha_{t+1} = alpha_t + gamma(alpha - 1{Y_t not in C_{1-alpha_t}}); long-run empirical miscoverage converges to alpha with probability one regardless of the data-generating process (their Theorem; stated by the review). — [Gibbs & Candes, arXiv 2106.00170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170) [Published, retrieved; guarantee formula via arXiv 2511.13608 review]
- Other conformal-for-time-series approaches exist: EnbPI (Xu & Xie, ensemble batch prediction intervals) and block conformal (Chernozhukov et al.); I saw titles/links only. — [Xu & Xie, arXiv 2010.09107](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.09107) [pointer only]; review of 2025-26 methods: [arXiv 2601.18509](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.18509) [pointer only]
- Brown, Cai & DasGupta (2001, Statistical Science 16(2):101-133) show Wald interval coverage is erratic even for large n and recommend Wilson or Jeffreys intervals for small n and Agresti-Coull for larger n; a secondary summary reports an example where Wald coverage for n=17 is 0.951 but for n=18 is 0.904. — [Brown, Cai, DasGupta](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-16/issue-2/Interval-Estimation-for-a-Binomial-Proportion/10.1214/ss/1009213286.full) [pointer only: primary blocked; claim via the secondary summary at [Paper Trail #43](https://tryvantage.co/insights/paper-trail-brown-cai-dasgupta-2001), a low-authority aggregator, so treat the n=17/18 numbers as unverified]
- Peaks-over-threshold with a generalized Pareto distribution (GPD) for extreme quantiles and declustering/extremal-index handling of dependent exceedances are covered in standard references (Coles 2001, *An Introduction to Statistical Modeling of Extreme Values*); a REVSTAT paper and a Springer chapter on POT surfaced in search but I could not read them. — [REVSTAT rs120105](https://www.ine.pt/revstat/pdf/rs120105.pdf) [pointer only: fetch failed]; [Springer POT chapter](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-60769-1_3) [pointer only]

### Inferences
- [Inference] For one-sided alarm thresholds, the quantity to calibrate is the (1 - p)-quantile of the in-control CUSUM/score distribution, or h directly for the chart. Two defensible procedures: (A) stationary bootstrap of the standardised in-control score series (or of its AR residual series), apply the chart to each bootstrap replicate of length >= several times the target ARL0, estimate the alarm rate per frame, and select h to meet the target with a bootstrap upper confidence limit; (B) direct empirical quantile of the per-frame score, with conformal-style finite-sample correction ceil((n_eff + 1)(1 - alpha))/n_eff, using n_eff not n.
- [Inference] Effective sample size: for a stationary series ESS = n / (1 + 2 sum_{k>=1} rho_k); for AR(1), ESS = n (1 - phi)/(1 + phi). [Background, unverified for primary source; standard Bartlett-type result; MCMC-diagnostic pages came up in search but were not read.] With n = 1000 frames at phi = 0.9, ESS is about 53; [Own arithmetic] the normal-approximation half-width on a rate near 0.5 widens from about 0.031 (n = 1000) to about 0.135 (n_eff = 53). At 10 Hz, 1000 frames is only 100 s, i.e., the 1000 frames may correspond to a few independent scenes. So ">= 1000 labelled frames" is a floor on frames only if frames are roughly independent; report the number of independent segments (corridor passes, scenes) and treat those as the sampling unit: bootstrap or cluster by segment/sequence (leave-one-sequence-out), not by frame.
- [Inference] The project's n >= 1068 rule for a Wilson half-width of +/-0.03 at p = 0.5 is simple arithmetic (1.96^2 x 0.25/0.03^2 = 1067.1); it assumes iid trials. Under dependence use n_eff in place of n or a cluster/block bootstrap interval for the rate. Wilson/Clopper-Pearson interval for a proportion are exact only for iid Bernoulli.
- [Inference] False-alarm rates at the target level are small, so the number of positives is what limits precision: to certify a per-frame false-alarm rate of 1e-3 you need many independent in-control frames. Rule of thumb (Clopper-Pearson zero-event upper bound, 3/n at 95%) [Background, unverified]: demonstrating a rate below 1e-3 with zero observed alarms needs about 3000 independent frames, not 3000 autocorrelated frames. Quote the ARL0 and false-alarms-per-hour with confidence intervals from the bootstrap.
- [Inference] POT/EVT for the 99th/99.9th percentile: choose a threshold u (e.g., 90-95th percentile) from mean-residual-life / parameter-stability plots, decluster exceedances using the extremal index, fit GPD(sigma, xi) by ML, and use x_p = u + (sigma/xi)[((n/N_u)(1 - p))^(-xi) - 1] for the p-quantile [Background, unverified: Coles 2001]. Only worthwhile when n_eff * (1 - p) >> 1 (at least tens of independent exceedances beyond u); otherwise the EVT extrapolation is mostly model assumption. For a hard-bounded statistic like l_min (bounded below by 0) the GPD with xi < 0 (finite endpoint) is natural; log-transform first.
- [Inference] Conformal under dependence, for this use: conformal thresholds control the marginal exceedance rate of the per-frame score; they do not control ARL0 of a sequential chart, because run length depends on the joint law. For a chart, use bootstrap/simulation to set h. ACI-style online updates (alpha_{t+1} = alpha_t + gamma(alpha - err_t)) are better suited to maintaining a long-run rate under drift, but need feedback labels (the geometric ground truth) online, which a deployed SLAM system does not have; use in offline calibration or periodic re-calibration only.
- [Inference] Held-out calibration protocol: split by sequence/environment (not by frames), calibrate (mu0, sigma0, AR coefficients, h) on calibration sequences, freeze, then evaluate false-alarm/ delay on test sequences from other environments; any random frame-level split leaks information through autocorrelation.

## 4. 'Information too low' as an equivalence / non-inferiority test; choice of H0; RAIM precedent

### Takeaway
Because a missed degeneracy is the costly error, the fail-safe formulation is H0 = "geometry is inadequate (degenerate)", declare "adequate" only on evidence (a lower confidence bound on information above the requirement) — the structure of an equivalence/non-inferiority test, using the intersection-union logic of TOST — with "unknown/insufficient evidence" mapped to the conservative mode. Integrity monitoring in GNSS (RAIM) is not literally that, but its availability check (protection level vs alert limit, an assessment that the geometry is good enough to detect faults at the required missed-detection probability) is the closest standard fail-safe precedent.

### Cited Findings
- Chi-square RAIM (fault detection): the test statistic is the squared norm of the parity vector, q^2 = p^T p = r^T r (r = least-squares residual), with (n - m) degrees of freedom, n measurements and m estimated parameters; under the fault-free hypothesis it is central chi-square; under a fault f it is noncentral chi-square with noncentrality lambda^2 = f^T Q^T Q f (Q the parity matrix). The threshold T is set from the continuity/false-alert allocation: P(q >= T | H0) P(H0) <= C_REQ,0. The integrity requirement is P(|error| > delta, q < T) <= I_REQ (alert limit delta); protection levels are probabilistic bounds on position error; the worst-case fault magnitude is found by line search. — [Joerger et al., Fault detection and exclusion using solution separation and chi-squared ARAIM](https://www.aoe.vt.edu/content/dam/aoe_vt_edu/people/faculty/joerger/publications/Fault_Detection_and_Exclusion_Using_Solution_Separation_and_Chi_Squared_RAIM.pdf) [Published, retrieved as model summary. Note: the summary itself said "central" in a heading and quoted "noncentral" for the fault-free case; the central-under-H0 reading is the standard one, treat the quoted sentence as a transcription slip]
- Equivalence/non-inferiority testing literature: equivalence tests reverse the null (H0: effect outside the equivalence bounds; reject to conclude equivalence), commonly via two one-sided tests (TOST; Schuirmann), formalised as intersection-union tests (Berger & Hsu 1996, Statistical Science 11(4)); Lakens' primer covers practical use. I could not retrieve these pages (blocked / captcha), so no quoted detail. — [Berger & Hsu 1996](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/statistical-science/volume-11/issue-4/Bioequivalence-trials-intersection-union-tests-and-equivalence-confidence-sets/10.1214/ss/1032280304.pdf); [Lakens, Equivalence tests primer (PMC5502906)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5502906/); [tests for equivalence/noninferiority between two proportions](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1177/009286150003400225) [Published, pointer only]

### Inferences
- [Inference] The two formulations differ in which error is controlled at level alpha:
  - H0 = adequate, reject when evidence of degeneracy (the CUSUM/RAIM-style detector): alpha is the false-alarm rate (unnecessary switch to vision / extra cost); the missed-detection rate is whatever power gives at the threshold, uncontrolled for subtle degeneracy and low in noisy frames. Natural fit when false alarms are the expensive error and the alternative is well specified.
  - H0 = degenerate, reject when evidence of adequacy (equivalence/non-inferiority style): alpha controls the probability of declaring adequate when it is not, i.e., the dangerous error; noisy or small-N frames yield "cannot reject" which defaults to degenerate (fail-safe), at the price of more false alarms in noisy frames.
  For a mode switch whose failure mode is silent drift in LiDAR-only mode, fail-safe default is the second (or a combination), but this is a design judgement, not a theorem.
- [Inference] Concretely for T: the information requirement is l_min >= c (c from pose-error data/geometry, e.g., the value at which translation error along v exceeds a tolerance). Declare adequate iff the lower (1 - alpha) confidence bound of l_min (block/cluster bootstrap over spatial cells) exceeds c, i.e., a one-sided non-inferiority test of H0: l_min <= c. Because the eigenvalue is a non-smooth functional, use a bootstrap lower bound rather than a delta-method SE, and check the nominal coverage by simulation on synthetic scenes with known l_min (cluster-bootstrap CIs for l_min can be anti-conservative when l_min is near the crossing with l_2).
- [Inference] Combine with sequential monitoring: run the equivalence-type test per frame to produce a three-state output {adequate (LB > c), degenerate (UB < c), uncertain}, then a CUSUM on the signed margin (e.g., z_t = (log LB - log c)/s_t) for switching with hysteresis. Uncertain frames count as degenerate for safety or as zero-increment frames depending on cost asymmetry; this must be tuned on data.
- [Inference] Which is standard for fail-safe detectors? In RAIM the detection test itself is H0 = fault-free with threshold from false-alert probability (so the formal test is of the first type, the Neyman-Pearson-style structure), while safety is secured by a separate availability determination: the system is declared unavailable (not usable for the operation) when the protection level exceeds the alert limit, i.e., the system must demonstrate adequate geometry/detectability (via the noncentrality needed for the required missed-detection probability) to be used. That "must demonstrate adequacy" step is the fail-safe component and it is structurally the same as the second formulation. I did not retrieve a primary source for DOP thresholds or the protection-level formulas (e.g., HPL = slope_max * sqrt(lambda_threshold-related) * sigma) [Background, unverified: Brown 1992; Walter & Enge; Joerger/Pervan]. The honest summary: both appear in integrity monitoring, in different roles.
- [Inference] The non-centrality idea transfers: define the minimum detectable degeneracy for the chosen chart (the smallest sustained drop in log l_min for which the detector achieves a required detection probability within a delay budget, given ARL0); report it. This plays the role of RAIM's minimum detectable bias, and is a more honest performance statement than a bare ROC point.

## 5. Evaluation protocol: geometry-defined ground truth, ROC/PR, McNemar, delay metrics, ARL0 estimation, calibration/ECE

### Takeaway
Define ground truth from geometry and from pose-error outcomes that the detector never sees, split by sequence/environment, evaluate at the operating point that calibration fixed, and report (i) detection rate and false-alarm rate with cluster-aware CIs, (ii) detection delay distribution, (iii) ARL0/false alarms per hour from held-out in-control data, (iv) paired comparison against the old detector with McNemar on discordant segments, (v) calibration/ECE only if the detector outputs probabilities.

### Cited Findings
- Expected calibration error (ECE): partition predictions into M confidence bins and take the weighted average gap between accuracy and confidence in each bin; reliability diagrams visualise it. — [Guo et al., On calibration of modern neural networks, ICML 2017 (arXiv 1706.04599)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.04599) [pointer only: I saw the paper link and title in search; ECE definition is from my knowledge of that paper, not read this session]
- Project protocol (not independent literature): geometry-defined Gazebo ground truth, Wilson CIs with n >= 1068, NEES calibration, held-out threshold sweeps, forced-mode ablations, GEODE/SubT-MRS datasets, and the stated weakness of the earlier validation (ground truth = detector threshold, circular). — [project doc SLAM_Degeneracy_Final_Methods_With_Proofs.md, D9] [project document]
- The ACI, Wilson/Brown-Cai-DasGupta and stationary-bootstrap findings from Section 3 apply directly to this protocol (interval construction, block length). — see Section 3 citations

### Inferences
- [Inference] Ground truth (never from the detector): label a frame/segment "degenerate along v" from (a) geometric ground truth: in simulation, the known scene geometry, giving the true information matrix/eigenvalue from the true surfaces with a known pose; in real data, independent measurements such as a map-based or ICP-with-reference Fisher information, or (b) outcome-based: reference-trajectory pose error along v exceeding a tolerance when running LiDAR-only, with reference from RTK/mocap/total station and alignment (e.g., Sturm et al. 2012 ATE/RPE, cited in the project doc as [Published] but not retrieved here). Keep the labelling threshold on the geometric quantity distinct from the detector's threshold to avoid circularity; sweep the label threshold as a sensitivity analysis. Label at segment level (e.g., contiguous runs) and exclude a guard band around transitions where ground truth is ambiguous, or score the transition region separately.
- [Inference] Unit of analysis: segments/sequences, not frames. Frame-level metrics inherit autocorrelation (Section 3), so use cluster-bootstrap or leave-one-sequence-out CIs. Report frame-level ROC/PR for ranking scores (AUC with sequence-bootstrap CI) but pick the operating point by fixing the false alarm rate per hour on in-control data (the ARL0 requirement), not by maximising F1. PR curves matter because degenerate frames are usually the minority class; ROC AUC is insensitive to prevalence while precision depends on it; state the prevalence.
- [Inference] Delay metrics: for each true degeneracy onset (from the geometry labels), detection delay = first alarm time minus onset time, with censoring when no alarm occurs before the segment ends; report median, 90th percentile, and miss rate (fraction of onsets with no alarm) using survival-analysis (Kaplan-Meier) summaries to handle censoring. Compare with ADD/CADD from sequential analysis theory: Lorden's worst-case delay ess sup E_nu[(T - nu)^+ | F_nu] and Pollak's conditional delay (Section 1) are theoretical analogues; the empirical metric is a sample average over actual onsets, not the worst case. Also report recovery delay (time to return to "adequate") because the switch must go back.
- [Inference] ARL0 estimation: with a held-out in-control set of total duration D (hours) containing a alarms, the alarm rate is a/D; the CI should come from a cluster/stationary-bootstrap over segments, since alarm counts are overdispersed relative to Poisson (alarms cluster in specific scenes). Run-length distribution is approximately geometric so the ARL0 estimate's relative SE is about 1/sqrt(a) for independent runs [Inference/Background]; to estimate an ARL0 of 3000 frames to +/-20% needs about 25 independent in-control run lengths, i.e., about 75,000 frames (about 2 hours at 10 Hz) of in-control data, if dependence is handled by resampling. If real in-control data is insufficient, supplement with bootstrap-resampled replicates (Section 3) and state that those ARL0 numbers are model-based. Do not report the Siegmund-formula ARL0 as an evaluation result.
- [Inference] McNemar's paired test: for old (e.g., fixed threshold l_min < 0.15) vs new detector on the same segments, build the 2x2 table of agreement with the label (both correct, old-only correct b, new-only correct c, both wrong) and test b vs c with chi2 = (|b - c| - 1)^2/(b + c) (continuity-corrected) or the exact binomial on discordant pairs [Background, unverified: McNemar 1947]. Assumption: pairs independent; autocorrelated adjacent frames violate this and inflate significance, so pair at the segment level (one decision per segment) or cluster bootstrap the difference in rates. McNemar compares accuracy at a fixed operating point only; pair it with an ROC/PR comparison (DeLong or bootstrap) for the score.
- [Inference] Calibration/ECE only applies if the channel emits a probability of degeneracy (e.g., from a logistic or conformal map of the score). Compute ECE and reliability diagram on held-out sequences with bin counts adjusted for the effective sample size, also report Brier score / log loss, and show sharpness. For a hard binary switch, a calibration table of observed degeneracy rate vs score decile is more useful and avoids ECE's binning artefacts. Re-fit the score-to-probability map (isotonic/Platt) on calibration sequences only.
- [Inference] Consolidated actionable defaults.
  - Per frame (online): compute T and l_min; compute y_t = log l_min; standardise with frozen (mu0, sigma0); optionally AR-prewhiten residual e_t = y_t - sum phi_j y_{t-j}; update matrix-EWMA of T and a one-sided downward CUSUM S_t = max(0, S_{t-1} + (-z_t) - k) with k about delta/2; also output a within-frame uncertainty (bootstrap SE over spatial cells, only if affordable; otherwise a cheap proxy such as the number of independent patches) and the weak eigenvector v.
  - Offline calibration (held-out in-control sequences, split by environment): estimate mu0, sigma0, AR order/coefficients; choose delta from geometry/pose-error analysis; select block length b by Politis-White; stationary-bootstrap the in-control residual series and set h to meet the target ARL0 or false alarms per hour with a bootstrap upper confidence limit; optionally add a GPD tail fit to the per-frame score to sanity-check the 99.9th-percentile extrapolation when n_eff(1-p) is large enough.
  - Must be measured on the user's data: marginal distribution of log l_min in adequate scenes (skew, tails, bias with sensor/range/N), autocorrelation function and spatial-block dependence inside frames, effective sample size per scene type, the harmful-degeneracy delta, outlier frequency, how l_min depends on N and on scan pattern (a small-N bias in l_min is likely), and the achieved ARL0 on held-out sequences. Everything quoted above as a number from my simulation is for a unit-variance Gaussian AR(1) and illustrates mechanism, not the user's rates.
